Six things that matter

  1. Built by schedulers, for schedulers. The COO is a home health PT and the operations manager ran centralized HCHB scheduling for 10 years. Priority groups for requests, a call log, and drive-time blocks on the clinician’s calendar are details only field people would build. 0:00:53
  2. Capacity is visible, not calculated for you. Points by clinician by day, colour-coded, on a map. A branch leader can enter an address and discipline and see who could take a new referral. There is no branch-level total and no forecast. 0:43:13
  3. The request workflow is the product’s best feature. Priority groups (full-time first, per diem after a delay), reminders, a timestamped read-and-response log, clinician comments, and an optional auto-assign to the first taker. It replaces the Teams-and-email scramble with data. 1:16:23

Process flowAs CareStitch runs it · click a stage

Configure, see capacity, assign or request, coordinate

CareStitch separates the task, the work the EMR requires, from the visit, the appointment that fulfils it. The office assigns tasks, directly or through a request, and the clinician books the visit and phones the patient. HCHB stays the record for documentation and completion.

00 · ConfigureLive0:22:18 – 0:33:46

Everything the agency knows, made into filters

What it does

  • Provider types (RN, LPN, PT, PTA, OT, COTA, MSW, HHA, SLP and others) and visit types with point values, deadline and threshold hours. For example, SOC is 2 points with a 48-hour deadline and ROC is 1.5.
  • General visits such as meetings, case conferences and breaks block clinician time, with a Teams link attached if wanted.
  • Custom properties, self-service: patient labels shown to the care team (e.g. “hospitalized” appears on every care-team phone at once), or matching properties set on both patient and clinician (PICC line, facility, language) that filter out ineligible clinicians automatically.
  • Facility matching lets an agency keep two nurses per building for continuity and the facility relationship.

For Compassus

  • Michael asked about labels set automatically from data flowing in. That is live for some EMRs’ patient-flag columns, and their API (in development) could accept labels from our intake platform. 0:28:28
  • Colin saw emergency-planning value: find at-risk patients by area in hurricanes or fires. Their clients have done this for wildfires.
  • All of it is configured by agency administrators with no vendor involvement. Governance across 80+ branches is on us.
01 · TerritoriesLive0:33:46 – 0:39:29

Territories drawn to the street, not the ZIP

What it does

  • Regions are built from ZIP codes or drawn polygons, to split a big ZIP, follow a highway, or carve up a dense city.
  • Regions can overlap; a clinician can belong to several; or a region can be one clinician’s personal territory (useful for choosy per diems).
  • Once set, the system knows a patient’s region from the address and which clinicians serve it. Nobody needs to carry territory knowledge in their head.

For Compassus

  • Michael raised Napa, where one town, ZIP and county forces Google-mapping every patient. Polygons fix that.
  • Colin asked for census and caseload overlays on territories. They were promised on the map view but not shown as a territory-level analysis.
  • Built-in territory knowledge is what makes a centralized scheduling model workable.
02 · CapacityLive0:39:29 – 0:44:44

Can we take this referral? Look at the grid

What it does

  • The schedule calendar shows each clinician by row and the Medicare week Sunday to Saturday, with points still open in bold. Grey marks days off. The map shows clinician homes and this week’s patients.
  • Each clinician has expected points per day. Assigning a visit deducts its points, so “no more mental gymnastics.”
  • To check a referral: enter the address, filter by discipline, employment type and properties (e.g. PICC line), and see who has room on which day.
  • Shapes on the grid mark whether a task has been booked as a visit.

For Compassus

  • This answers common question 1 at the clinician level. There is no branch total, no forecast and no referral-inflow view. The leader adds it up by eye.
  • Michael wants to query this from the intake tool: “do we have someone who can see this patient?” CareStitch’s API is in development. 0:47:11
  • Pending-auth demand can only be shown with a custom property; auth isn’t ingested.
03 · AssignLive0:44:44 – 0:49:42

Direct assignment with the context in view

What it does

  • Select the patient and the disciplines needed. The grid shows each eligible clinician’s distance for each day, from this patient to their nearest visit that day or to their home.
  • A yellow asterisk marks a clinician who has seen the patient before, for continuity.
  • The scheduler picks, the tasks go to the clinician, and a push notification reaches their phone.

For Compassus

  • No recommendation or ranking. The system filters and displays; the scheduler judges. Their return calls this “advise and orchestrate.”
  • Evan asked whether plan-of-care data from intake could fill in the disciplines and visits automatically. It would take the future API, and today it is clicks. 0:48:06
04 · RequestLive1:16:23 – 1:37:13

The staffing request: an offer that finds its taker

What it does

  • Build a request: patient, visit type, deadline (e.g. SOC within 48 h), discipline, gender or language preference, notes (“dialysis, afternoons only”).
  • Eligible clinicians are sorted into priority groups (full-time, part-time, per diem, contractor), each with a configurable delay and reminder. The scheduler can deselect someone (“in documentation jail”) or move them to a later group.
  • Clinicians see the visit, its distance from their home and from each booked visit, and the deadline. They reply available or not available and can add a comment (“only 2pm Friday”).
  • A queue colours each request: red when the deadline is close and nobody has said yes, blue when someone is available. A timestamped log shows who was sent it, who read it and who answered.
  • Assignment can be manual (favour full-time or closest) or automatic to the first clinician available. If nobody takes it, add recipients from other territories.

For Compassus

  • This replaces the Teams, email and text scramble with data we don’t have today: response speed and accept rates by clinician.
  • Colin: branches cherry-pick visits in Teams that should go together. This structure stops that.
  • Colin saw uses beyond visits: on-call, therapy weekend rotations. Their clients already do this with staffing requests.
  • The first auto-assign version was reworked because agencies wanted control. Pick the default deliberately.

05 · Book the visitHuman-led0:49:42 – 1:14:24

The clinician still calls, but with better tools

What it does

  • The mobile app home shows today’s appointments and a productivity summary (points to target). Patients are sorted by the next task due.
  • The patient-centred calendar shows the clinician’s other appointments with distance from this patient, a drive-time overlay that greys out travel time, and other disciplines’ visits for this patient in red, so the PT sees the SOC is Thursday.
  • Click-to-call any number on file, with an automatic call log and notes the office can see.
  • The office side has an Optimize Route button for centralized scheduling. Clinicians place visits one at a time around ones already confirmed, anchoring the fixed ones first.

For Compassus

06 · RecoverLive1:14:24 – 1:16:23

A call-out, reassigned by hand

What it does

  • Open the clinician who called out and reassign each visit. Eligible clinicians are pre-filtered by discipline and enough points for the visit.
  • Widen the search to PTAs; see each candidate’s distance to the patient and to their booked visits; save.
  • If nobody fits, send it as a request (stage 04). Clinicians can also start a reassignment request from their phone.

For Compassus

  • It works and it is quick, but it is manual per visit, with no suggested replacement, no ranking and no bulk accept.
  • No home health guardrails. Asked about SOC-before-eval when dates shift: “we don’t currently have those guardrails… the guardrails live in the system of record.” LUPA is roadmap. 1:44:09
07 · CoordinateLive1:37:13 – 1:44:09

Every patient gets a care-team room

What it does

  • A secure chat room is created for each new patient, and every clinician assigned joins it automatically. The office is included, and newcomers see the full history.
  • Channels: broadcast (office speaks, field reads) and restricted (clinicians post, only the office sees). Plus direct messages and groups.
  • Chat history exports per certification period, for interdisciplinary-communication evidence in audits.
  • Sub-organizations: a clinician working for several branches or lines shows one combined availability.
  • Staffing requests double as a ticket queue for pending admissions and other office work.

For Compassus

  • This is the strongest coordination layer of the three vendors. Compare with Teams, where the scramble happens today.
  • Evan asked for scheduler productivity reporting (who touched what, how long). Not built; “never been asked.” 1:52:21

ConsiderationsRanked by how much they would change the decision

What to weigh

High

Automation is not built

At 340 schedulers and 10,000 clinicians the value is in automation. CareStitch has the workspace and is only researching what goes on top of it.

High

HCHB integration

A read-only beta by screen automation that has never run in production, with no write-back. It was not discussed on the call.

High

No home health logic

No order, auth or frequency ingestion; no SOC-before-eval, LUPA or window enforcement. The scheduler and the EMR carry it all.

Medium

Patient calls stay with clinicians

Our biggest single ask is roadmap: confirmation first, negotiation later, no dates. Click-to-call and a call log help, but they don’t remove the work.

Medium

Clicks at scale

Every assignment and reassignment is manual. What helps one scheduler becomes a cost across 340.

Medium

Scale and proof

The largest customer has 5.3k patients in one branch; most outcomes are anecdotal. We would be the enterprise proof point.

Medium

Configuration sprawl

Self-service properties and priority groups are powerful, and across 80+ branches they will drift without central governance. That fits our standardization goal only if we enforce it.

In their favour

Uptime and offline mobile

No outage in 12 months; clinicians keep the day’s work when offline. The best continuity story of the three.

In their favour

Field-built, coordination-first

The request workflow, care-team chat, drive-time and call log are the best coordination tooling seen so far. The team knows HCHB and the branch reality.

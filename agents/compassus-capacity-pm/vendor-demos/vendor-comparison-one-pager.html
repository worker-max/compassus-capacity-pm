Three vendors, one page

Axle Health, CareStitch and VitalisCare scored on how much of our Variable Inventory each call covered, how far each product goes toward deciding for us, how much scheduler work each could remove, and what changes for the clinician.

Axle leads on every dimension that moves the business case, but its go-live is gated by our 3-hour HCHB feed. CareStitch has the best clinician and coordination tooling and no automation yet. VitalisCare is a hospice engine that would need home health built on top.

The three things that decide value

Our judgment from the demos · five-point scale
AxleCareStitchVitalisCare
Product sophisticationHow far it goes from showing data to recommending and deciding

Recommends all three decisions
Ranks clinician, day and time with every factor shown; call-out cover suggested; zip-level forecast. Can run autonomously; customers keep a person in the loop.

Filters and displays
Removes ineligible clinicians, then the scheduler judges. The request workflow orchestrates outreach well. Recommendations and AI are in research.

Recommends who and when
Ranked provider matches and better-day suggestions with an AI rationale; overrides need a reason. Exceptions engine not shipped; no home health logic.

Scheduler task reductionHow much of the 340 schedulers’ work it could take away

High, if the data flows
Work queues to zero, bulk-out of the episode in one pass, a call-out in seconds. Claims −74% scheduler time per visit, fully realized centrally. Needs HCHB faster than 3 h.

Medium: coverage, not assignment
Kills the Teams and email coverage scramble and territory lookups. Every assignment is still a click. One customer doubled census with no new scheduler.

Low to medium
Matching and day suggestions help, but plotting stays in HCHB, call-off recovery isn’t built, and a 3 h lag invites double entry in HCHB.

Clinician experiencePerceived improvement for the clinician in the field

Less evening phone time
Optimize and finalize the night before; patients get a confirmation text instead of a call. Blocks with addresses. Opt-in rollout. The “why” goes to managers, not clinicians.

Built by clinicians
Drive-time overlay, click-to-call with log, other disciplines’ visits, care-team chat, offline app, 93% adoption. But clinicians still phone every patient.

Mixed, with a trust risk
Routed day that reroutes live, but GPS workday tracking and route-adherence review, a second app beside PointCare, and no patient contact.

What each vendor must prove at the on-site

The question that decides each one

Axle

  • A joint data plan: intake feed for new patients, a target HCHB cadence for orders, and a date to turn write-back on.
  • The shared scenario run end to end, including LUPA pacing and a 10am referral decision.
  • Partnership terms, company size and a 5k+ ADC reference.

CareStitch

  • A dated HCHB production plan, read and write.
  • The automation R&D they offered to show, and when recommendations ship.
  • A date for patient text confirmation, and a 60-day episode on the shared scenario.

VitalisCare

  • Written scope, dates and funding for the home health build: auth, episode and LUPA, therapy, engagement.
  • The Call-Off Center running live, including when nobody takes the visit.
  • Security and uptime (skipped on the call), size and funding.

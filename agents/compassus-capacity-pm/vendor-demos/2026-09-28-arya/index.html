Process flowAs Arya runs it · click a stage

Pick up, confirm, recommend, staff, write back

Arya treats every piece of care as a capacity window: the span inside which a visit must happen. It then searches each eligible clinician’s calendar for the best slot inside that window. The office and the clinician stay in the loop by approval or by text. HCHB stays the system of record, and Arya writes changes back to it.

Rules per branch, in two layers

What it does

  • Entities. Each branch is an entity. The agent inherits rules set at the organization level, and each branch can override them for its own shortages, overtime, or rural and urban driving.
  • Guardrails are hard requirements: licensure (an RN for a SOC), on-call and PTO calendars, a productivity-point ceiling, maximum travel time (e.g. 90 minutes), minimum time between visits, and hard patient preferences such as gender, smokers or pets.
  • Heuristics rank the eligible clinicians: total drive time for the day, preferred days and hours, full-time before PRN, prior experience with the patient (last 30 days), and patient and caregiver preferences.
  • The demo tenant: one mocked HCHB instance, five home health entities, 20 synthetic clinicians (RN and LVN, full- and part-time), and three visit types: SOC, standard recurring and wound care.

For Compassus

  • We decide which rules are hard and which are soft. “If you think clinicians should not have a say on which regions they go to, that becomes a hard requirement.” 0:36:15
  • Branch-level override fits our branch variation. It also means 80+ rule sets that can drift apart; central ownership of the defaults is our job.
  • Continuity counts among the heaviest heuristics, and Arya says it can be made a hard rule. It will then report how often no clinician qualifies (“agent health”). 1:16:36

03 · StaffLive0:29:18 – 0:33:41 · 1:24:57 – 1:29:24

Office approves; the clinician confirms by text

What it does

  • In co-pilot mode the office accepts, rejects or puts a recommendation in review. Alerts can go by Teams, text or email.
  • A demo rule: accept a full-time clinician and Arya writes the SOC to HCHB at once. Accept a part-time/PRN clinician and it waits: Arya texts them a link to approve the patient first.
  • Other setups: Arya texts or calls eligible clinicians in batches, best fit first (“not blast everyone and their uncle”). Clinicians apply, and the office sees applicants with their coverage (e.g. “Fenwick Delacorte, 88.5%, 15 pts, part-time”).
  • Later the office moves from approving to being informed. It sees only the critical items: “No clinicians seem to be available. I need you to figure out if anyone can take this case.”

06 · RecoverLive0:55:17 – 0:58:48 · 1:39:10 – 1:48:13

A call-out is one text; the agent re-forms the week

What it does

  • The clinician texts “I can’t work Wednesday.” Arya lists the three visits and asks which to keep or move. The reply is “I can’t work any of these.”
  • Arya first tries to move the visits within the same clinician’s week, inside each visit’s capacity window. If it can’t, it raises call-outs, re-staffs them, and tells the office.
  • Applicants come with Arya’s own recommendation, e.g. “Tami Stephenson covers 3 of 10 visits with a 0% match”, and with reasons a clinician isn’t eligible (“Not eligible for 1 of 1 visit”).
  • For continuity it can favour the clinician who covers most of the episode: “Antoinette Delice covers 8 of 33 visits with a 47.1% match.” Visits past the authorization window aren’t scheduled; Arya escalates, and Put in review holds the slot while an expected auth comes through.

Every “no” is data; every rollout needs champions

What it does

  • Rejection with a reason. The office rejects a recommendation and types why (“Quilla doesn’t go to the city at all,” typos included). Arya decides whether this is a clinician, patient or organization preference and applies it next time.
  • Insights. Shifts filled, hours staffed, caregivers engaged, open shifts. Applications by status, actions by staff member, and a rejection breakdown (“Why staff overrode Arya”).
  • Agent health. An outreach funnel (21 eligible, 6 contacted, 6 applied, 1 approved) and eligible clinicians per shift. The funnel shows whether the rules are too tight to staff anyone.
  • Change management. A branded “Welcome to Arya” training hub with “Ask me anything,” and a Compassus-branded rewards program: gift cards for day-one staffing and weekly challenges.

ConsiderationsRanked by how much they would change the decision

What to weigh

High

Screen-automation integration

Citrix RPA at 15–20 minutes, SSO logouts, and HCHB setups that differ between customers. An agent that acts on a 20-minute-old schedule can double-book. It must prove itself on our sandbox at our volume.

High

Capacity view not shown

Branch-level supply against expected demand, at the moment of accepting a referral, is what we are buying. Arya described it and did not show it.

High

Governing learned rules

Free-text feedback becomes rules. The tool to see and edit “the brain” is still being built. Across 80+ branches, who approves a rule a clinician taught the agent?

Medium

Autonomy boundary

Full-time assignments can write straight to HCHB, and approval is framed as a phase. Our Assist boundary needs approval as a permanent option, per action.

Medium

Proof, not promises

No measured result. Outbound voice and batch outreach were described but not shown live, and the applicant data had low match scores.

Medium

Patient-facing AI

Our clinical operating leaders may limit it. The outbound number, language support and escalation rules all need clinical sign-off.

Medium

Demo data hygiene

During the demo, the presenter’s inbox showed what looked like a live escalation from another customer, with a caregiver’s name and a patient’s initial (0:33:39). Ask how demo and customer data are kept apart. That screen is not included here.

In their favour

The whole loop, live

The only vendor to show referral → patient → clinician → HCHB write-back working together, with a suggested time as well as a person.

In their favour

Built around the clinician

Text instead of an app, preferences set at onboarding, the clinician’s yes before anything lands, and a change-management playbook that matches ours.

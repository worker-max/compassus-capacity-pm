Process flowAs Axle runs it · click a stage

Who, what day, what time, then recover

Axle splits scheduling into three decisions, which clinician, which day and what time, and lets each organization choose who owns each one. Their default: the office picks the clinician, the office or the clinician picks the day, and the clinician picks the time. Capacity sits above the whole flow, and patient texts go out at the end.

Your rules, inferred from your own people

What it does

  • Assignment logic from a conjoint survey: your staff choose between clinicians in sample scenarios, and Axle infers the logic you actually use. You then review it and tune it during the pilot.
  • They recommend fewer than a dozen factors, ideally six or seven, set up as a decision tree rather than weighted scores. That keeps every recommendation explainable.
  • Services map one-to-one to HCHB service codes, each with a duration, priority, disciplines and licences.
  • Territories: a soft default per team or per clinician. A clinician can still be scheduled outside it.
  • Shifts and blocks with addresses and recurrence. The route optimizer plans around a mid-day appointment at its actual location. An “out sick” shift starts a call-out.

For Compassus

  • Workday integration isn’t built. They reviewed the APIs and want to pull availability from there instead of asking clinicians to enter it twice. 1:26:33
  • Evan wants mandatory weekly availability from every clinician. Axle advised making it optional at first: it matters only where someone other than the clinician sets times. Evan’s point stands, and this is a design decision for us to make.
  • Productivity points by service code and targets by licence can be imported or kept in Axle.

01 · Data inPaused for HCHB0:16:40 – 0:34:20

The integration they built, and why it’s off

What they said

  • Read from the HCHB log ship: patients, service codes, clinicians, visits, frequencies and orders, auth and tasks. Write visits and tasks back by RPA, or by computer-use agents as those mature.
  • The bar: a new SOC must reach Axle within about 10 minutes. HCHB’s fastest refresh is 15 minutes, and when a refresh runs slow HCHB simply skips that cycle. Their Epic customers get a reliable 15 minutes, which is tolerable because it is predictable.
  • They won’t go live on data they can’t time. HCHB’s move to Databricks is the long-term fix; Compassus warned that HCHB “still can’t get off Citrix.”
  • Suggested splitting the refresh by object: orders and frequencies fast, heavy documentation slow.

For Compassus

  • New patients: our intake platform (built with Commure, HL7 and APIs possible) can feed demographics and ordered disciplines in real time. That was enough for Axle to create the patient and episode and generate visits.
  • Routine visits still depend on HCHB. Frequencies are written in the field and plotted by a scheduler workflow in HCHB, so at 3 hours Axle can’t recommend days for new orders promptly.
  • Compassus has cut refresh from 6 hours to 3 once already, and the data architecture work is the lever. Axle offered to think it through with us.
  • RPA is the only write path into HCHB. Compassus is comfortable with it across several initiatives.

“We won’t actually go live with somebody if we can’t give any data guarantee… we need to be able to tell them when they will be in Axle.”Adam Stansell, Axle · 0:20:01

02 · Work queuesLive0:49:45 – 0:56:00

The scheduler’s day starts at inbox zero

What it does

  • A home page of queues you can configure, each one a gap to close: incomplete care team, SOC, ROC, evals, urgent visits, patients needing scheduling, pending therapy re-evals, upcoming end of episode, visits scheduled before the SOC, conflicting discharge and recert visits, completed visits missing an order, active patients with no visits, missed visits.
  • A calendar live view alongside shows today’s visits by attention status.
  • Other roles get their own queues: clinical managers work missed visits, the office tracks transfers. Clinicians get a slimmer task list in the mobile app.

For Compassus

  • Several queues are home health compliance checks we raised with other vendors, built in here: eval before SOC, therapy re-eval, recert against discharge.
  • Evan asked about reporting on queue close-out by scheduler. The data is there, but a report wasn’t shown.
03 · Which clinicianLive1:47:54 – 1:49:36

A care team per discipline, with every factor shown

What it does

  • For a new patient, Axle recommends the care team, one per discipline (RN, PT, OT, HHA and so on), with the factors laid out as a colour-coded table: care-team match, team, territory, top of licence, capacity, employment type, distance, caseload.
  • It accounts for PTO and how full each clinician already is. It prefers top of licence (an OT over an OTA) and the less expensive licence where equivalent.
  • Care team first; then every visit inherits it unless someone overrides.

For Compassus

  • Clinician-facing rationale is deliberately limited. Clinicians see the reasoning for decisions they own (day, time). Assignment reasoning goes to the scheduler or manager, to avoid “why me and not Evan?” disputes. That runs against Colin’s wish for clinicians to see why. 1:07:41
  • Axle sees the scheduler as “a salesperson for capacity,” with the data needed to persuade a clinician to take a patient.
04 · Which dayLive1:49:36 – 1:50:36

Bulk out the episode inside each visit’s window

What it does

  • Every visit carries a fulfillment window, such as the Medicare week Sunday to Saturday. Date recommendations pick the best day within the window while optimizing the whole episode.
  • The rules shown were spacing between visits of the same order (no two SN visits on one day for a 2w8), front-loading early in the week, keeping a patient’s usual weekday, and grouping patients geographically.
  • Episode-level gaps such as a recert in the last five days are flagged even when no visit exists yet (from their return).

For Compassus

  • This is the step our HCHB scheduler workflow does by hand today, and the one the 3-hour data lag hurts most.
  • LUPA thresholds weren’t shown on screen. Evan named LUPA and PDGM management as value levers; ask to see LUPA pacing on the shared scenario.
05 · What timeLive1:50:36 – 2:00:08

Optimize, finalize, and the patient is told

What it does

  • The route optimizer sequences a day, or many clinicians’ days, around shift blocks, patient constraints and facility hours. Usually the clinician runs it the night before: optimize, then finalize.
  • Finalize sends each patient an SMS with an arrival window rather than a minute. The clinician works in sequence, not clock times.
  • Patients have no app. Texts run through Twilio under a BAA, and replies route to the clinician’s authenticated mobile app. Voice and email are also available.
  • Patient availability starts fully open. Anyone can add constraints, standing (“dialysis Fridays”) or for a single visit.

For Compassus

  • Their stance is confirm, don’t ask. Asking patients “can I come at 9?” loses the 9am slot, so negotiation moves to the prior visit and replies go to the clinician. It cuts the 30–60 minute evening call round without handing scheduling to a bot. 1:54:34
  • Evan’s north star is an agentic AI that captures patient availability and reschedules. Axle has self-scheduling in production but advises against it; if we use it, collect when patients are unavailable, not when they are free.
  • Mobile app not shown live; videos and training guides promised.

“It shouldn’t be a phone call… but it also shouldn’t be an automated text bot where they can’t push back.”Adam Stansell, Axle · 1:57:15

06 · RecoverLive1:42:58 – 1:47:54

A call-out, handled in seconds

What it does

  • Mark a shift “out sick” and the clinician’s visits open in a reassign flow, one visit at a time. Choose: keep the date and change the clinician, or keep the clinician and change the date. Their return adds a third option: switch the service code (an LPN visit to RN).
  • Every option comes with its reasoning shown: territory, licence cost, capacity, employment type, distance, caseload, spacing.
  • Accept all, submit, and the visits move. The covering clinician gets a push notification by default; for a PRN, the scheduler can call first.
  • If nobody fits, Axle still names the best option and prompts the office to call (from their return).

For Compassus

  • This is the only live run of common question 6 in the field so far.
  • They front-load within the week, so a missed Monday can land later in the week. Colin’s concern about brittleness was answered with front-loading and sequence over exact times.
07 · Capacity & resultsLive0:56:00 – 1:25:00

Capacity by zip, and proof the logic works

What it does

  • Projected utilization by clinician territory and zip: historical demand, trends and seasonality set against actual capacity (PTO, reduced hours). It serves three audiences: recruiting (hire in the Valley), clinical ops (move a clinician over), marketing (don’t chase referrals where you’re full).
  • Checks on the assignment logic: continuity of care, top-of-licence efficiency, employment-type mix, gender and language match, miles from home.
  • Clinician stats: mileage, on-time rate, completion, call-out rate, patient ratings, and points utilization for the last and next four weeks against target.

For Compassus

  • Michael described the intake platform as the place where marketers, intake, insurance and ops come together. What’s missing is capacity insight at accept or decline. Axle wants to feed and take in our marketing forecasts both ways. “A blank canvas conversation.” 1:25:01
  • Common question 1 (the referral decision) came closest to an answer here, though a 10am referral wasn’t walked through live.

ConsiderationsRanked by how much they would change the decision

What to weigh

High

Go-live depends on our data

Axle won’t launch on a feed it can’t time. The intake feed covers new patients; routine orders need faster HCHB data. The pilot date is really a data-architecture date.

High

Write-back into HCHB is unproven here

RPA or computer-use write-back exists but has no live HCHB customer. Detection, repair SLA and conflict handling for a customer our size are all untested.

Medium

Patient engagement philosophy

Confirm-don’t-ask is proven but conservative. Our agentic two-way vision would be layered on top of it, and Axle will push back. Decide which we want before the on-site.

Medium

Clinician visibility of the “why”

Assignment reasoning goes to schedulers and managers by design. Colin wants clinicians to see it. It can be configured, but it goes against their advice.

Medium

Scale

At 24k ADC we’d be about twice their largest deployment. Performance of the logic and data volume at our size need proof on the pilot.

Medium

Commercials unasked

Partnership, size and funding weren’t covered. The return’s “referral credits and discounted features” is thinner than the design-partner investment we’re offering.

Medium

Workday and availability

No Workday integration yet. Evan wants mandatory availability; Axle wants optional adoption. That must be settled in the pilot design.

Medium

Location tracking policy

By default Axle tracks only trips to a patient, when the clinician starts one. Whole-shift tracking is available, and choosing it is a privacy and HR decision for us.

In their favour

Home health fluency and candor

80% home health, our vocabulary unprompted, a live recovery flow, change management learned the hard way (“rip the band-aid off” was a mistake), and they switched their own integration off rather than ship a poor experience.

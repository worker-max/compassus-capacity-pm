Six things that matter

  1. It is a hospice product, and they said so. “Our Sync tool has been developed primarily for hospice.” The demo used hospice disciplines (aide, social work, chaplain), hospice compliance alerts (face-to-face, RN supervisory gaps) and hospice CMS benchmarks. 0:23:57

  3. HCHB freshness is the real constraint, and it is on our side. Their typical client ships HCHB logs every ~10 minutes. Compassus ships every ~3 hours, which they called too stale for call-off recovery. Writes back through RPA take seconds to minutes. 0:46:46
  4. Advise-not-decide is a principle, not a phase. Every recommendation is accept-or-dismiss. Overrides require a reason, which feeds the model. This fits our Assist boundary and our clinician culture. 0:59:04
  5. Exception handling is still a design, not a product. The Call-Off Center, which makes the 3-hour lag survivable, is “in design, slated for release before the end of the year.” It was shown only as slides. 1:04:48
  6. The clinician app is a GPS tracking app. Sync360 monitors the workday, reroutes in real time and powers a performance review of reported versus tracked mileage. It is powerful for oversight and a real adoption question for tenured clinicians. 1:13:48

00 · ConfigureLive0:17:51 – 0:27:00

Rules set once per branch and discipline, then per person

What it does

  • Patient preferences as free text (language, days, hours, discipline). They can be entered by schedulers on the web or by clinicians in the app, and feed the algorithms.
  • Care provider profile: weekly hours, weekends, long-term-assignment eligibility (to keep per diem or on-call staff out), date overrides and PTO.
  • Branch settings in the Account Portal: distance bands (demo branch: close ≤ 23.9 mi, far > 39.1 mi), daily visit quotas per discipline, RN-or-LPN substitution for SN orders, and matching scope (the patient’s branch, associated branches, any branch or a custom set).
  • New-worker ramp: how long someone counts as new (default 2 weeks) and how fast their load ramps. Full-time and part-time hour definitions. Standard weekly schedule per discipline.

For Compassus

  • Colin asked whether new clinicians can be kept off specific visit types such as SOC. Not today; ramp is by productivity only. They offered to add it. 0:25:36
  • With 80+ branches, per-branch configuration is real implementation work. Ask who configures it and how long it takes.
  • The settings list is hospice-shaped: it includes music therapy, massage and Reiki, and no PT, OT or SLP defaults were shown.

02 · WhoLive0:32:52 – 0:44:30

Care Provider Matches: ranked long-term assignment per discipline

What it does

  • A newly admitted patient appears in a queue. Sync shows the top four providers for each discipline (the hospice default is aide, RN, social work and chaplain). It can be limited to disciplines with open orders.
  • There are two lenses. Best Mapping ranks on location and caseload geography first. Smart Choice balances location against capacity and productivity, and most customers use it.
  • Each card shows a productivity %, a caseload-fit map with service span in miles, compatibility chips (branch, team, language, prior encounter, same facility) and a short AI rationale.
  • An override requires a reason. That reason is both a feedback loop into the model and an audit trail for leadership.
  • Submit moves the patient to Pending while RPA writes the assignment to HCHB. Sync polls until the write is confirmed.

For Compassus

  • Territories: Colin asked about ZIP-based territories. Sync uses teams and branches, not territories. If we don’t encode territories in HCHB, they would need to understand how we categorize them. 0:36:43
  • Clinician-facing reasoning: Colin asked whether clinicians see the “why” behind an assignment outside their usual area. Not built; Vitalis liked the idea. 0:41:27
  • Productivity points can be shown against target, which Paige called “a really great touch.”
03 · Write backLive0:44:30 – 0:54:36

HCHB in by log shipping, out by RPA

How it works

  • Read: the HCHB log ship arrives on SFTP, loads into a full database, and a subset of fewer than 100 of HCHB’s thousands of tables is refreshed every ~10 minutes. Very large clients run Vitalis’s subset script on their own side. One client streams from an HCHB database mirror.
  • Write: RPA (software robots working HCHB’s screens), which Vitalis calls “very robust” and uses across products. Seconds to minutes per write.
  • Vitalis is in partnership talks with HCHB for 2027. Today they rely on log shipping.

For Compassus

  • Minimum cadence was not committed. “It depends on what you expect.” One client is fine hourly; others say 10 minutes is too slow. Reassignment is where it bites.
  • Double entry is the failure mode. Schedulers who don’t trust the lag redo the change in HCHB and create conflicts. That makes adoption discipline part of the integration design.
  • Compassus has cut HCHB refresh from 6 hours to 3 for another AI project and will explore going faster. A broader data modernization “probably solves all of our problems” but takes a while.
  • Not asked: what happens when HCHB changes a screen and the RPA breaks. Ask on the second call. The same fragility was flagged for Arya’s screen automation.

04 · WhenLive0:54:36 – 1:04:42

Schedule optimization, one suggestion at a time

What it does

  • A week grid per clinician flags visits that aren’t on their best day. For example, it moves Powell, Warren from Thursday to Wednesday because the provider is already at that facility that day. Each suggestion shows the reasons and that day’s caseload map.
  • Accept once or recurring, or decline. Manual drag-and-drop warns on out-of-window visits, expired licenses and same-day duplicates.
  • The provider panel shows the 30-day caseload map, visit frequency and certification alerts (yellow when expiring, red when expired).
  • The original HCHB schedule can be viewed side by side. There is also PTO entry, export for facility communication, and a Notification Center of compliance alerts (hospice: face-to-face and RN supervisory gaps).
  • Schedule Full reshuffles a whole month for support staff. Vitalis prefers small incremental shifts that move caseload centroids over time.

For Compassus

  • Hospice frequencies are weekly or monthly. There was no SOC window, recert window or LUPA threshold in the logic shown. Compassus raised LUPA directly; Vitalis said it is “flagged… a gap.” Front-loading logic already exists and could be reused. 1:24:45
  • Home health compliance alerts (OASIS timepoints, recert, missed-visit risk) would all be new.

“Retaining that power balance with your schedulers making final decisions empowers them rather than paralyzes them.”Dina Yankelewitz, CEO · 0:59:04

05 · ExceptionsIn design · EOY1:04:48 – 1:13:48

Call-Off Center: the piece that makes the lag survivable

As described

  • A queue ranked by urgency. It pulls visits from PTO, HCHB and PointCare declines or late statuses, and weighs visit windows and compliance (hospice HOPE visits, for example).
  • It recommends either rescheduling with the same provider or replacing them. Trade-offs are shown: productivity, drive time, overtime risk. It keeps the same replacement across a multi-day absence for continuity.
  • It writes back to HCHB and pushes the change to the clinician’s phone at once.

For Compassus

  • Their slides contradict each other. The capacity slide marks Exceptions as “Live”; the roadmap slide says ranking and escalation “are still in development.” Believe the roadmap slide.
  • Common question 6, a Tuesday that goes wrong, was described but not run live. “What happens when nobody takes the visit” was not answered.
  • Compassus said it does not want a third-party call center (Vitalis’s term for the Call-Off Center) “right out of the gates” but wants all the functionality. Clarify what that means before the next call.
06 · Day ofLive1:13:48 – 1:17:30

Clinician mobile (Sync360)

What it does

  • The clinician starts, pauses and ends the workday; GPS monitors visit completion only while the workday is on.
  • Today’s visits come as a routed list with a map, and hand off to Google Maps or Waze. The route recalculates in real time as visits are added or removed.
  • Today, week or month views; patient details; adding patient preferences; notifications of schedule changes; setting their own working hours.
  • Time off comes from HR systems (iSolved integration live; Workday to be explored) so nothing is entered twice.

For Compassus

  • Clinicians would still document in PointCare, so this is a second app (VitalisCare question 8). Declining a visit from the app depends on the Call-Off Center.
  • Compassus’s north star is no work inside HCHB and one screen for schedulers and clinicians. Vitalis says no other organization has asked for that. “We’ve been begging for that.”
  • Colin pressed on day-before confirmations: 4–5pm calls and texts, replies at 9pm. The app has nothing for this yet. See the engagement row below.
07 · OverseePartly live1:36:30 – 1:50:24

Live map, performance review and predictive benchmarks

What it does

  • Live Map (real data, names blurred): where every clinician is now, their progress through the day, the closest provider to a patient, untracked providers and tracking gaps.
  • Care Provider Performance: visits per day, average start time, flags when a device is switched off, and a colour-coded triage. Each day can be reviewed for reported versus tracked mileage, time and duration mismatches, and route adherence on a map.
  • SSVI Predictor (designed, not live) tracks CMS hospice service benchmarks.

For Compassus

  • Territories are the gap. Colin wants a branch-director view of patient and provider caseload by territory that shifts with referral patterns, because territory is “the start of capacity.” The live map is day-of only. Vitalis: “it wouldn’t be hard to build.” 1:40:42
  • The tracking and discrepancy layer is framed as accountability. For tenured, salaried home health clinicians it needs deliberate change management and probably HR and legal review.
  • SSVI and the benchmarks are hospice measures. There is no HHVBP or OASIS outcomes equivalent.

ConsiderationsRanked by how much they would change the decision

What to weigh

High

We would be the home health design partner

Authorization, episode and LUPA, therapy and engagement all need building. The upside is influence and speed. The cost is that our team specifies it and our branches find the bugs.

High

Data cadence is ours to fix

The 3-hour HCHB log ship undermines same-day recovery. Options: a faster ship, a subset script on our side, a database mirror, the data modernization program, or their HCHB partnership in 2027.

High

Exceptions are not shipped

The Call-Off Center is the component that makes everything else work with lagged data. It is due before year end, and slides disagree on its status.

Medium

RPA write-back fragility

Robots working HCHB’s screens break when HCHB changes. We didn’t ask about detection, repair SLA or production incidents.

Medium

GPS tracking and clinician trust

Route adherence and mileage discrepancy reports are oversight tools. For salaried, tenured clinicians they are a change-management and HR question as much as a feature.

Medium

HCHB still in the loop

Visit plotting stays in HCHB with intake. Scheduler workflow tasks pile up unless auto-cleared (planned). This conflicts with our north star of no HCHB touch points.

Medium

Scale and durability unknown

3 live organizations and ~6–7k patients against our footprint. Company size, funding and headcount were not covered. We would likely be their largest customer by a multiple.

Medium

Security and continuity unpresented

Architecture & Continuity was on their agenda and skipped for time. Uptime, SOC 2 and their HIPAA posture are open.

In their favour

Philosophy that fits us

Advise-not-decide, reasons on every override, and incremental change over big reshuffles. That matches our Assist boundary and discovery findings on clinician adoption. The team was candid and quick.

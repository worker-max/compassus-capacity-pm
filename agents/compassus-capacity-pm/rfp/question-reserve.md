# Question reserve — held back from round 1

> **Internal. Not sent to vendors.** The round-1 questionnaire is capped at 20 questions plus the
> coverage matrix, so it reads as a serious but answerable ask. Everything below was drafted, judged
> valuable, and deliberately deferred. Each entry says **where it should land** and **what it is
> for**, so the virtual calls and the Nashville sessions start from a prepared bank rather than a
> blank page.

## Why these were cut

Three tests decided what stayed in round 1:

1. **Does it separate vendors on paper?** Anything most vendors would answer identically is wasted
   in writing and better live.
2. **Is a written answer trustworthy?** Demo-shaped questions ("walk us through the screen") produce
   marketing prose on paper and real information on a call.
3. **Does the coverage matrix already answer it?** Part B covers all ten sub-areas. Any question
   that only re-asks "do you do X" was redundant against the table.

## Stage 2 — virtual calls (~6 vendors)

These are the questions that need a screen, a follow-up, or a tone of voice to be worth anything.

### Capacity

- How does availability and time off actually get into the system, who maintains it, and how current
  is it in practice? *(Their answer to "who maintains it" tells you whether it decays.)*
- Show us the seven-day capacity view. What decision is it designed to support, and who looks at it?
- How is visit weighting calibrated, and what happens when a customer's weighting is wrong?
- How does geography affect whether a clinician can serve a patient — territory, drive time, or
  straight-line distance? *(Straight-line is a tell.)*

### Scheduling

- Describe your routing capability and the data behind it. Live traffic, historical drive time, or
  distance?
- How do you handle constraints that belong to the clinician versus constraints that belong to the
  patient or caregiver — and where do those inputs come from?
- How does your product distinguish timing that comes from a clinical order or regulation from
  timing that is a preference?
- What exactly happens when a scheduled visit does not happen? Walk the states.

### Engagement

- How and when do you capture a patient's availability, and what do you do when it conflicts with
  clinical need?
- What happens when a patient wants to change a visit after it is booked?
- How do you handle communication consent, opt-out, language preference, and the regulations on
  automated outreach? *(Matters in specific markets — California and Washington especially.)*

### The clinician

- Walk us through what a clinician sees and does in your product across a typical week. **Demo, not
  prose.**
- How do you measure clinician adoption, what do you consider healthy, and what does your data show
  about the first six months?
- What does your product do *for* clinicians, beyond the organization's efficiency goals?

### Commercial

- **Pricing and commercial model.** Deliberately absent from round 1: asking early anchors the
  negotiation and invites a sales response rather than a product one. Ask once the field is down to
  six.
- What does implementation actually require from us — people, time, and from which roles?
- What does your support model look like after go-live?

## Stage 3 — Nashville deep dives (top 3)

By this point we are showing more of our own thinking, so these can be specific about our operation.

- The full variable inventory, walked area by area against their product.
- The three ceilings on an episode — authorization as permission, LUPA as the floor, utilisation
  management as the ceiling — and how their product would represent each.
- The day-before negotiation: hard constraints versus soft preferences, and whether the product can
  tell them apart.
- Our current-state flows in detail, including the per-discipline task explosion and the DCS
  approval and lock mechanics.
- Recertification and discharge: how their product handles an episode ending in staggered,
  per-discipline discharges.
- Co-development in concrete terms — what a design-partner arrangement would look like, who does
  what, and on what timeline.
- Data model and extensibility: can we get our own data out, and can we build on top?
- Failure modes: what happens when their system is down on a Monday morning?

## Questions we decided not to ask at all

- **Anything asking them to price or scope our specific deployment.** Too early; it produces a sales
  artifact rather than a product answer.
- **Feature checklists.** The coverage matrix does this better and faster, and a checklist hands
  vendors our vocabulary.
- **Anything that states our position on clinician autonomy.** Every vendor would agree with it, the
  answers would converge on nothing, and we would have given away a stance. Part D describes the
  *environment* — clinicians choose home health partly for control, and adoption decides success —
  and then asks questions where experience shows and marketing does not.

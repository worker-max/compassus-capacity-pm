# Scheduler Insight Session — Agenda & Question Bank

> **Goal:** extract the tacit "tricks" 4–5 experienced schedulers use to manage capacity day-to-day, structured
> so each insight can become **tool automation** or **AI-agent behavior**. Runs the
> [SME discovery framework](../sme-discovery-framework.md) against the scheduler lens; validates/extends the
> [scheduler SME brief](../perspectives/03-senior-scheduler.md) and the [tactics library](../capacity-tactics-library.md).
> **Default length: 90 min** (scales to 60 by cutting Themes E/G; to 120 by adding a live walk-through).

## Before the session

- **Invite the right mix:** 4–5 schedulers across a **high-performing branch and a struggling one**, and if
  possible one **newer** scheduler (they still remember what's confusing) + one **10-year veteran** (most
  tricks). Different markets (urban/rural) if you can.
- **Send a light pre-read** (1 line): *"We're building a tool to kill your busywork and make your judgment go
  further — come ready to tell us the tricks that keep your branch running. No prep needed."*
- **Ask for one artifact each:** a screenshot/photo of their actual scheduling grid or the report they live in
  (redact PHI). The real artifact is worth an hour of abstract talk.
- **Optional pre-observation:** shadow one scheduler's morning (7–9am) and end-of-day beforehand; bring what you
  saw as prompts.
- **Roles in the room:** 1 facilitator (asks, keeps it concrete), 1 scribe (captures to the schema below), 1
  quiet observer (watches for the throwaway lines — that's where tricks hide).

## Framing (say this out loud, 3 min)

> "You are the experts; we are not here to check your work or replace you. We're building a capacity tool to
> take the repetitive parts off your plate and make your judgment reach further. The most valuable thing you can
> give us today is the stuff that **isn't in any SOP** — the shortcuts, the who-to-call instincts, the tells you
> read. There are no wrong answers, and 'here's the workaround I'm not sure I'm supposed to do' is exactly what
> we want. **Tricks amnesty** — nothing said here gets anyone in trouble."

Two ground rules that make it work: **keep it concrete** (real last week, not "in general"), and **it's okay to
disagree** — where two great schedulers do it differently is a real design decision, not a wrong answer.

## Block 2 — A day in the life (warm-up, 10 min)

Get them concrete and talking before the hard questions.

- Walk me through **yesterday morning** from the moment you sat down. What's the first thing you check?
- Where does your time actually go — what eats the most minutes in a day? *(Listen for the 50–60/day auth
  notifications, manual grid updates, phone tag.)*
- What's the difference between a **good day** and a **day from hell** on your desk? What makes it go sideways?
- If you disappeared for a day with no backup, what would break first?

## Block 3 — The tricks round (55 min, ~7 min per theme)

For each theme: **lead with a specific scenario**, then probe, then always ask the two closing questions:
**(a) "What's your trick there — the thing you do that a new scheduler wouldn't know?"** and **(b) "If a smart
assistant could do one part of that for you, what part?"**

### Theme A — Reading *real* capacity (not the calendar's white space)
- When you look at a clinician's day, how do you know if they're **actually** available vs. just having a gap?
- What looks like open time but isn't? *(PTO, on-call recovery, documentation time, a patient who won't take
  early visits.)*
- Once a visit's been accepted and the patient's been told — can you still move it? How do you handle that?
- **Trick probe:** how do you spot the clinician who *says* they're full but isn't, or vice versa?

### Theme B — The last-minute referral / add-on order
- It's **Friday at 3pm** and a SOC comes in for a full clinician's ZIP. Walk me through *exactly* what you do —
  first move, second move, third.
- Who do you go to first, and why them? Does it change if nursing is on the case vs. therapy-only?
- When do you stop trying to place it and escalate?
- **Trick probe:** what's the move that saves a placement everyone else would've bounced?

### Theme C — Cancellation & discharge backfill
- A visit cancels at 9am. What happens next — and how fast do you *have* to move before that slot is dead?
- How do you decide what fills a freed slot — and who?
- What makes a freed slot get wasted?
- **Trick probe:** how do you catch cancellations fast enough to do anything about them?

### Theme D — Per-diem / PRN management (the coordination pain)
- How do you keep per-diems engaged so they pick up when you need them? What makes one go quiet?
- How far ahead do you know you'll be short — and what do you do with that lead time?
- When you need a per-diem, how do you decide **who** to ask and **how** to ask so you get a yes?
- **Trick probe:** what's your trick for a per-diem who's drifting away — how do you win them back?
- *(This is the discovery's named hard problem — spend extra time here.)*

### Theme E — Territory & routing
- How do you keep a clinician's day from turning into a driving day? What's your rule of thumb on distance?
- When a new visit comes in, how do you decide whose "loop" it fits into?
- Where are your branch's coverage holes — the ZIPs that are always a problem?
- **Trick probe:** how do you cluster a day so it just flows?

### Theme F — The "ask" that gets a yes (and when NOT to ask)
- Think of a clinician who almost always says yes. Why? What have you (or the branch) done to earn that?
- When you *have* to ask someone to stretch, how do you frame it so it lands?
- Who do you **not** ask, and when? *(On-call recovery, someone you tapped yesterday, someone having a hard
  week.)*
- **Trick probe:** how do you keep from burning out your most reliable people?
- **Fairness probe:** how do you spread the extra work so the same person isn't always carrying it?

### Theme G — Discipline balance / offload (do they see it?)
- Do you ever notice an RN drowning in routine visits an LPN could do? What do you do about it?
- Same for PT/PTA — how do you decide what a PTA can carry vs. what the PT has to keep?
- When you're short at one level, how does it show up on your desk?
- **Trick probe:** what tells you the branch is mis-staffed before anyone official says so?

### Theme H — Upstream friction (what breaks their day from elsewhere)
- What comes to you from **intake** in a state you can't act on? What's missing when a referral lands?
- How much of your day is waiting on someone else — DCS, auth, a clinician's documentation?
- When a clinician says "my schedule is a mess," how often is it actually *your* doing vs. something stuck
  upstream?
- **Trick probe:** how do you work around the handoff that's always broken?

## Block 4 — Automate vs. never (12 min)

The bridge from insight to product.

- If this tool could do **one thing** to give you back time tomorrow, what would it be?
- What part of your job is pure mechanics that a computer should just *do*? *(Filling the grid, chasing auth
  notifications, computing who's open.)*
- What part could a smart assistant **suggest**, but you'd want the final call? *(The ask, the reassignment.)*
- What should a tool **never** do on its own — where would it get it dangerously wrong or make a clinician feel
  jerked around?
- If the tool proposed "assign this SOC to Maria," what would you need to see to trust it — and what would make
  you instantly distrust it?

## Block 5 — Wishlist & wrap (8 min)

- Finish this: *"I could take 30% more referrals if only I had ______."*
- What's the one number or view you wish you could see that you can't today?
- Anything we didn't ask that we should have?
- **Parking lot:** capture anything raised that needs a follow-up or a different SME.

## What to capture (scribe schema)

Log every insight in the tactic-capture shape so it feeds the tool + agents directly (see the
[discovery framework](../sme-discovery-framework.md)):

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| **Trick / insight** | What they actually do (their words where vivid) |
| **Trigger** | When it applies |
| **Why it works** | The mechanism |
| **Automate as** | The rule / signal / view the tool could build |
| **Keep human** | The judgment part the agent should only *suggest* |
| **Never** | Any "the tool must not do this" guardrail |
| **Open number** | Any threshold they named (miles, days, %, notice hours) — we need real values |
| **Disagreement?** | Where schedulers differed (= a design decision, not an error) |

## The "open numbers" to try to pin down

Push gently for real values on these (they route into the strategy's parameters-to-validate):
- Max acceptable **drive time / distance** added to a clinician's day before it's "too far."
- **Notice** a clinician needs for a same-day/next-day add before it's an ambush.
- How many **days of quiet** before a per-diem is "drifting."
- How **fast** a cancellation has to be caught to be reusable.
- What "**full**" actually means to them (visits? points? a feeling?) — and what they *wish* it meant.
- **How many** last-minute placements / cancellations they handle on a typical day (volume for sizing).

## After the session

1. Within a day, write up each insight in the schema above while it's fresh.
2. Mark each: **confirms** an existing tactic / **corrects** one / **new**.
3. Promote high-confidence, high-frequency tricks into the tool backlog + agent-training corpus.
4. Feed disagreements and open numbers back to the strategy's open-questions list.
5. Flag anything that's really an **upstream** problem (intake/DCS/auth) — schedulers will surface these, and
   they're the discovery's core finding, not a scheduling fix.

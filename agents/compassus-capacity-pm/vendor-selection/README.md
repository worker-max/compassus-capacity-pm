# Vendor Selection — Capacity & Scheduling Platform

The evaluation record for the capacity-and-scheduling vendor search. Eleven vendors returned the
questionnaire, were scored against the v3.0 rubric, and six advanced to two-hour operational-design
calls. **This directory is the memory of that process** — the rubric's mechanics, what each vendor
actually said, what the field as a whole failed to answer, and the questions built to settle it.

## The five things to carry forward

1. **The total is a sort key, not the decision** — the rubric says so itself, and the shortlist
   proves it. Two vendors advanced from below the Consider band. That is legitimate, and it means
   **every exception needs its reasoning written down or the shortlist cannot be defended later.**
2. **HCHB integration is the axis the field splits on.** Live and bi-directional (Arya, HCHB,
   VitalisCare) versus built-and-switched-off (Axle), read-only beta (CareStitch), or absent
   (MedArrive and all five excluded vendors). Nothing else separates the field so cleanly.
3. **Nobody demonstrated payment fluency.** PDGM, LUPA, OASIS and recert are absent from almost every
   return. Axle used the vocabulary unprompted; MedArrive earned it in a different business; Zeeva
   has it with no customers. **This is the field's shared blind spot and the demo's sharpest test.**
4. **Clinician fit is the weakest scored section across the entire field** — 0.25 to 0.75, nobody
   higher. Several returns describe a feedback loop and never describe the moment a clinician says no.
   This aligns exactly with knowledge-base finding #6: change management, not technology, is the risk.
5. **Engagement is the thinnest arena** and the one most often sold as roadmap. Patient-facing work
   is absent or staged late almost everywhere.

> Vendor returns are commercial submissions, not verified fact. Every score in this directory is a
> Compassus reader's judgment of a claim, recorded with its reasoning. Treat the marks as reads, not
> measurements — and treat anything a vendor asserted about a third party (notably HCHB's own
> latency and roadmap) as unverified until HCHB confirms it.

Scoring the vendor questionnaires

Sixteen vendors have returned the questionnaire. Two ways to get from those to a shortlist — the same judgement, at two levels of depth.

1

The full scorecard

ABOUT 10 MINUTES A VENDOR

Each questionnaire is scored out of a hundred across the five things we said mattered. The team picks from menus; the sheet does the arithmetic.

  • 25 Home Care Home Base integrationDo they already connect to the system our plans of care, orders and visits live in? Ticked from a list of six, not judged.
  • 30 Scope coverageHow much of the scope we published they actually cover — reported separately for capacity, scheduling and engagement.
  • 20 Product qualityWhether they explained how the product decides, or only that it exists.
  • 10 Clinician fitWhat the clinician still controls, and whether adoption is proven rather than promised.
  • 15 PartnershipWhether they will trade on the investment we are making.

Home Care Home Base is a quarter of the score and a tick box rather than a judgement, so the priority cannot get quietly watered down as we work through sixteen vendors.

Scope is measured against the one-pager we published, so each vendor is compared to what we asked for rather than to one another's marketing. Everywhere else, the score rewards a vendor who showed us how their product decides over one who simply says it does.

What comes out is a number we can defend line by line, plus three short lists for each vendor: what makes them different, what worries us, and what they did not answer.

2

The simple scorecard

ABOUT 3 MINUTES A VENDOR

The same judgement at a coarser grain — twelve rows a vendor instead of fifty.

  • Home Care Home BasePicked from the same list of six.
  • Eleven areasThe ones each vendor already rated themselves on in the questionnaire.
  • Two marks eachWhat they have, and how good it is out of a hundred.
  • It adds upA coverage percentage and a rating for capacity, scheduling and engagement.

It follows the functional scorecard already in our workbook, so it reads like something the team has seen before. Most of it is transcription rather than judgement: what a vendor has comes straight from their own answer.

This is the fast read across the field. What it cannot do is separate two vendors who both cover an area but do it very differently, and it carries no partnership or clinician score at all — so where the shortlist is close, option one is the one that holds up.

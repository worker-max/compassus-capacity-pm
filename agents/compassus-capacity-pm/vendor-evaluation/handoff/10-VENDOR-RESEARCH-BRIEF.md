# 10 · Vendor research brief

**For:** the session researching each of the sixteen vendors from public sources
**Read first:** `00-START-HERE.md` §1–4 and `01-INITIATIVE-BRIEF.md`. You do not need the rest of
the pack to research, but you need to know what the questionnaire asked and what the scorecard
rewards, so read `02-QUESTIONNAIRE.md` and `03-SCORECARD.md` once.

---

## 1. Why the research exists

The questionnaire is the vendor describing themselves. The research is the outside view. It exists
to test what they claimed, and to inform three parts of the scorecard that a questionnaire cannot
settle on its own:

- **A1, the integration rung.** Is the HCHB integration real, live, and theirs? Partner listings,
  press releases, customer references and job postings all leave traces.
- **A2, scale and the Durability intangible.** How many customers, how large, how funded, how old,
  and how much of their business is home health. *Are we uncomfortably their largest customer?*
- **Who wrote this.** Who the company actually is: founders, leadership, engineers with home health
  backgrounds, or a general logistics product with a healthcare landing page.

It never scores. It feeds the notes column and the demo agenda.

## 2. Rules

1. **Public sources only.** Company site, press, funding databases, job boards, conference talks,
   app-store listings, patents, court records, LinkedIn-visible leadership, customer case studies,
   HCHB partner pages, state business registries.
2. **Every fact carries a source and a date.** A URL or a named document, and when it was published
   or retrieved.
3. **Not found is written as *not found*.** Never inferred, never filled from a similar company.
4. **Separate what they say from what others say.** A vendor's own case study is a claim. A
   customer's conference talk is evidence. A job posting is evidence of what they are building.
5. **No contact with the vendor.** No sign-ups, no demo requests, no emails.
6. **One dossier per vendor**, written to
   `agents/compassus-capacity-pm/vendor-evaluation/research/<vendor-slug>.md`. One session per
   vendor is preferred; if several are done in one session, finish and save each before starting
   the next.
7. **Do not read the vendor's questionnaire return before researching**, if you have it. Research
   first, then compare, so the outside view is not shaped by the inside one.

```
## What the outside view says about the questionnaire
Three to six lines. Where public evidence supports, contradicts or is silent on what a vendor of this
shape would be expected to claim on A1, A2, A3, C6 and E2. Written before reading their return; a
second pass after reading it is added below, dated.

## Durability read — evidence only
The facts that bear on: will they exist in three years; will this still be their main business; are
we their largest customer by a multiple. No verdict.

## Questions the research raises
For the demo or the reference call. One line each.

## Not found
What was looked for and not found, so nobody looks again.

## Sources
Numbered list. URL or document, title, date published, date retrieved.
```

## 4. Where to look, in order

1. The company site: about, team, careers, customers, partners, press, security or trust page.
2. Funding and company databases for stage, rounds, investors, headcount.
3. Home Care Home Base partner and marketplace pages, and any press naming both companies.
4. Job postings, twelve months: what they are hiring for says what they are building and where
   they are thin.
5. Conference programmes and recordings in home health and post-acute care: customers speaking
   about the product are the best evidence there is.
6. App stores for the clinician app: version history, ratings, reviews.
7. State business registries and court records.
8. Customer press releases and annual reports naming the vendor.

## 5. What to watch for

The same traps the questionnaire reader watches for, seen from outside:

- A home health landing page on a product built for a different industry.
- Case studies with a percentage and no period, baseline or site count.
- *Integrates with HCHB* on the site, with no partner listing and no customer confirming it.
- Headcount falling while the customer list is not growing.
- One large customer that is most of the company.
- Leadership from sales and marketing with no one from operations or clinical practice.
- The product renamed or repositioned in the last two years.
- The company acquired, or for sale.

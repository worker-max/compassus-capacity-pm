## One paragraph

UnityAI is a three-year-old Nashville company, founded in 2023 by three former HCA Healthcare data
scientists, that sells voice- and text-based AI agents to do front-office work in **multi-site
outpatient clinics**: booking, confirming, intake, follow-up, and since April 2026 a staffing module
called StaffOps that matches clinic staff shifts to appointment demand. It started as a hospital
bed-flow product, pivoted to outpatient in 2025, and has raised about $15M across a pre-seed, a $4M
seed (February 2024) and an $8.5M Series A (March 2026). It has roughly twenty to twenty-two staff,
claims about 350 sites live and 300,000 patient interactions a month, and names two customers:
Tennessee Oncology and Peregrine Health, a behavioural-health company serving FQHCs. Nothing in the
public record places it in home health, home care or hospice, and nothing connects it to Homecare
Homebase.

## What the outside view says about the questionnaire

Written 2026-09-05, before reading the return.

- **A1.** Expect no live HCHB integration. There is no public trace of HCHB, no named EMR of any kind, and the company's integration hiring is current. A rung of *Will build* or *API-based, not yet live* would match the record; anything higher needs a customer to confirm it. Watch for **RF-01** (integration claimed without mechanism) and for the voice layer being described as theirs when it is Vapi's (**RF-03**, core scope through an unnamed partner).
- **A2.** Real customers exist, but in outpatient clinics. Compassus would be their **first home health customer**, and at roughly three thousand clinicians probably their largest by workforce, against a typical customer of 100 to 200 clinics. **RF-16** is the expected flag, on both halves: home health is a minority (in fact zero) of the business, and we would be their largest by a multiple. References, if offered, will be oncology, behavioural or dental.
- **A3.** Every public number is a percentage with no period, baseline or site count. Expect the same in the return and expect **RF-05**. The two scheduler-productivity figures (26% and 29%) are the kind of thing **RF-18** catches.
- **C6.** SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 are claimed publicly, which is above the field's floor. No uptime figure or SLA is public. If the return names neither, **RF-07** applies as written. Ask specifically about Vapi's availability, since an outage in the voice layer is an outage of the product.
- **E2.** Early-stage, venture-backed, seven institutional investors, $15M in, Series A closed six months ago. An equity or co-development conversation is structurally possible at this stage, and would need the board. Watch **RF-12** (a discount offered as partnership) and **RF-22** (the shortest answer is on ownership).

## Durability read — evidence only

- Company age: three years. Two repositionings in that time, from hospital bed flow to outpatient front office to staffing.
- Capital: $15M total; $8.5M raised 4 Mar 2026. Headcount about 22. No revenue figure public; Vapi describes revenue as growing "5X", period unstated.
- Main business: multi-site outpatient clinics, by specialty. Home health does not appear in any public material, past or present.
- Concentration: one unnamed customer has 300-plus sites out of a claimed 350; that customer is most of the footprint.
- Our size against theirs: about eighty branches, three thousand clinicians, three hundred schedulers, against a typical customer of 100 to 200 clinics and a team of about twenty.
- Dependency: the voice channel runs on a third-party platform (Vapi) that raised $50M in 2026 and lists UnityAI as a marquee customer.
- Leadership: three founders still in place; data-science and hospital-analytics backgrounds; no operator from home-based care.

## Questions the research raises

For the demo or the reference call. One line each.

1. Name every EMR you are live with today, and the customer who will confirm each one.
2. Which parts of the voice stack are yours, and which are Vapi's? What is the contract term, and what happens to live calls if it ends?
3. Have you ever integrated with a system that has no public API? Describe how.
4. Has any team member worked in home health, home care or hospice operations? Name them.
5. In StaffOps, what is the unit of supply: a shift at a site, or a person with a territory and a caseload? Show the data model.
6. How do your agents handle a schedule that the clinician builds themselves the evening before, rather than one the office builds?
7. Give the period, baseline, site count and method behind the 26% (or 29%) scheduler-productivity figure. Which is it?
8. Who is the 300-site customer, and will they take a reference call at scheduler level?
9. What happened to the hospital bed-flow customers, if there were any?
10. What is your uptime over the last twelve months, and what does the contract commit to?
11. What is your current runway, and what does the Series A fund?
12. What is the largest workforce you serve today, in clinicians?

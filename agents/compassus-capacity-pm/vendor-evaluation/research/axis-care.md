## One paragraph

AxisCare is a thirteen-year-old Waco, Texas company that sells a complete operating system to home
**care** agencies — scheduling, electronic visit verification, custom forms, billing and outsourced
revenue cycle management — to agencies billing private pay, Medicaid and the VA. It is the most
solid, most ordinary, most professionally-run company on this roster: two institutional investors
inside two years, a sixteen-person named leadership team, third-party review-site recognitions, and
a product that has grown outward from one thing it has done well since 2013. It is also, on the
evidence, in a different business from ours. Home care schedules an hourly shift for a client; home
health schedules a discipline-specific visit inside a Medicare episode against an authorization and
a visit frequency. AxisCare has a “Skilled Care” module and no Medicare on its payer list, no EMR in
its integrations marketplace, and no demand forecast anywhere.

**The decide-or-advise contradiction, in full.** AxisCare's own November 2025 press release describes
*Automated Open Visit Recommendations* as “a scheduling tool that automatically identifies and
**recommends** caregiver matches for open visits based on availability, skills, and client
compatibility”. That is our posture exactly. Seven months later, LLR's investment release describes
the same platform as “the System of Action for home care: an AI-native platform that doesn't just
record agency activity but **actively and intelligently drives** scheduling, compliance, and care
decisions.” **Same product, two postures, seven months apart, and the more aggressive one is written
for investors.** Which sentence the salesperson uses in the room is a test worth running.

## What the outside view says about the questionnaire

- **A1.** Expect either no HCHB claim or a roadmap answer. The outside view is **silent to
  contradictory**: there is an integrations marketplace and **no EMR is in it**. If the return
  claims a live HCHB integration, that is a direct conflict with their own marketplace page and
  should be the first question asked.
- **A2.** Expect a strong scale answer, and it will be honest: fifty states, seven countries, two
  institutional investors, thirteen years. **We would not be their largest customer** — probably the
  only vendor on the roster of which that is true. That is a real strength, and it is in the wrong
  market.
- **A3.** Expect the 280% figure. It has no baseline, no period and no unit.
- **C6.** Expect HIPAA and encryption. Expect **no SOC 2 and no HITRUST**, because none is
  published, and expect no uptime figure — they say they monitor uptime and publish no number. The
  ransomware paragraph explicitly declines to say anything: “While we don't disclose our specific
  measures online, we are well-prepared.”
- **E2.** Expect “recommends”. Watch for “drives”. Both are in their public record.

## Durability read — evidence only

The most durable company on the roster by a distance. Thirteen years old, profitable enough to
attract a minority growth investment in 2024 and a second institutional round in 2026, fifty states,
seven countries, sixteen named executives, third-party review recognitions in the current year, no
rebrand and no pivot. They will exist in three years and this will still be their main business.
Compassus would be a large customer but not a company-defining one. **The durability question does
not apply here. The fit question does, and it is severe.** Two institutional investors in twenty-six
months also means a firm on a growth-equity clock, with a likely third event ahead; ask who would
own the roadmap in three years.

## Questions the research raises

1. Medicare. Your published payer specialisms are private pay, Medicaid and VA. How many
   Medicare-certified home health agencies run on AxisCare today, and can we speak to one?
2. Your integrations marketplace contains no EMR. Have you ever integrated with a clinical system of
   record, and with which one?
3. What does the Skilled Care module do with an OASIS assessment, a PDGM period, a recertification
   window or a visit-frequency order? Show it, do not describe it.
4. Your November 2025 release says the scheduler *recommends*. Your June 2026 investor release says
   the platform *drives* care decisions. Which is shipped?
5. “Improve care oversight by 280%” — what is the unit, what was the baseline, over what period, at
   how many agencies?
6. Is there any forecast of demand or required staffing anywhere in the product?
7. SOC 2 or HITRUST — held, in progress, or neither? What is the contractual uptime commitment?
8. Who is the cloud provider, and which subprocessors touch PHI?
9. LLR came in June 2026 with Frontier still on the register. What is the expected hold period, and
   what happens to the product roadmap at the next event?
10. Is there a single clinician or home health operator in your leadership or product organisation?

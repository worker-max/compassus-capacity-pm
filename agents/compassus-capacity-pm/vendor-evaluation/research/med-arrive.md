## One paragraph

MedArrive raised about $40m between 2020 and 2023 to run a national field-provider network:
paramedics and EMTs visiting patients in the home on behalf of health plans, with telehealth
backing them up. That company no longer exists. In 2025 it “transitioned away from directly
providing care” and rebuilt itself as a software platform — a logistics and care-transitions layer
developed with ChristianaCare — its co-founder and chief executive stepped down around October 2025,
it bought the technology assets of Inbound Health (an Allina Health spin-out that shut down in
December 2025) in March 2026, named a new chief executive from Alto Pharmacy and TytoCare on the
same day, and moved its registered address from New York to Colorado. Its website was rebuilt in
August 2026 and now runs to ten pages, none of which names a customer, an EMR or a person. What it
sells is close in shape to what we need — predict who is coming home, coordinate the handoff,
schedule and route the visit — and almost nothing about it can be verified from outside yet.

**Why the pivot matters more than the usual pivot.** Every other repositioning on this roster is a
marketing decision — servis.ai renaming a CRM, UnityAI changing a product name three times. This one
changed what the company sells, who it sells to, who runs it, and which state it is in, and it did
so after spending roughly $40m proving the previous model. That is not a red flag by itself: the
people who ran a field network for five years know exactly where in-home logistics breaks, and that
is real domain knowledge of a kind almost nobody on this roster has. It is a **durability** fact and
an **A2** fact, and it means the reference customers, the production track record and the tenure of
the team all reset to 2025.

## What the outside view says about the questionnaire

- **A1.** Expect either no HCHB claim or a roadmap answer. The outside view is **silent**: no EMR of
  any kind is named on the site. If the return names HCHB, ask what was built, with whom, and when —
  because as of four weeks ago they were not saying it in public.
- **A2.** Expect care with the scale answer. The honest version is that the **current** product is
  about eighteen months old with one named development partner. Any customer count offered should be
  asked to separate customers of the software from customers of the old field-provider business.
- **A3.** Expect “30% more visits with the same staff” and “14x ROI”. Neither has a period, a
  baseline or a named site. The 30% figure is the single most relevant claim any vendor has made to
  our actual problem, which is exactly why it needs a denominator.
- **C6.** Expect a good answer, and this one is **corroborated**: “AICPA SOC II certified” appears in
  the privacy policy as well as the marketing. That is stronger than anything on the roster except
  AutoMynd. Ask for the report and its date, and note the policy names **no AI subprocessor** while
  the product markets AI-backed navigation.
- **E2.** Expect automation language. Their page says the platform matches providers to visits
  *automatically*. Ask where the human is.

## Durability read — evidence only

Six years old as an entity, eighteen months old as this business. About $40.5m raised, none of it
publicly since 2023, spent substantially on a model the company has abandoned. Founder-CEO gone
October 2025; new CEO March 2026 with a product background from Alto Pharmacy and TytoCare and no
published home health operating history. Registered address moved from New York to Colorado. No
published headcount. No named customer of the current product. Growth so far by buying the corpse of
a competitor that shut down three months earlier. Against all of that: a strong investor register
including two payer-affiliated funds, a real health-system development partner in ChristianaCare,
a genuine SOC 2 claim, and five years of first-hand operating knowledge of what breaks when you send
clinicians into homes — which is knowledge, not marketing. **The question is not whether they
understand the problem. It is whether the company that understands it is still the company that
would be selling to us.**

## Questions the research raises

1. What is in production today, at how many organisations, and since when?
2. ChristianaCare: is it a customer, a co-development partner, an investor, or all three? Can we
   speak to them?
3. Which EHRs do you read discharge and referral data from, by name, and by what mechanism?
4. Do you integrate with HCHB? If so, what does it move, in which direction, how often, and who
   built it?
5. “30% more visits with the same staff” — at which organisation, over what period, from what
   baseline, at how many sites?
6. What survived the transition away from direct care: which engineers, which code, which customers?
7. How many people work here now, and how many worked here in 2024?
8. Show the SOC 2 report and its date. Which subprocessors touch PHI? Which model provider powers
   the AI-backed care navigation, and is it named in any customer-facing document?
9. What exactly did you buy from Inbound Health, and is it integrated or still separate?
10. Your page says the platform matches providers to visits automatically. Where does a human
    scheduler intervene, and what is the default when they do not?
11. Has there been a further funding round since 2023, and what is the runway?

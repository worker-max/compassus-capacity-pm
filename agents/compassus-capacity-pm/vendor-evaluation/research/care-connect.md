## One paragraph

CareConnect is an eight-year-old, privately held, New York company that sells a caregiver
workforce platform to **home care agencies**: the aides who deliver hourly personal care, mostly
under Medicaid. Its core is a caregiver app and matching engine (ShiftMatch.AI) that offers open
shifts to aides by compliance, location, cost, availability, skills and travel time, and since
2024 lets aides pick their own shifts, with chatbots and auto-dialers doing the outreach. Around
that core it has bolted on an applicant tracking system, recruiting outsourcing, occupational
health, eLearning and a cross-agency caregiver-sharing pool, and in January 2026 renamed the whole
thing a Workforce Operating System. It was founded by Bert Brodsky, the founder and owner of
Sandata, the electronic visit verification company, and most of its named partnerships are with
Brodsky's other companies or with EVV and home care platforms. It has about ninety staff, a
leadership team hired from IntelyCare and AlayaCare since 2023, and reports revenue growth but no
revenue. No skilled home health customer is named anywhere, and nothing connects it to Homecare
Homebase.

## What the outside view says about the questionnaire

Written 2026-09-05, before reading the return.

- **A1.** Expect no HCHB integration and expect the word *integrates* to be doing a lot of work. The public integrations are single sign-on into a caregiver app and partner listings whose mechanism is not published. **RF-01** if the return claims bi-directional or real-time anything; ask what data moves, which way, and how often. Axxess is the only skilled-home-health EMR they have touched.
- **A2.** They almost certainly have many customers, but they are aide agencies, mostly New York Medicaid. **RF-16** on the first half: skilled home health is not their business. On the second half they may say we would not be their largest, and by headcount that could be true (Help at Home is large). The right question is not size but kind: name one Medicare-certified agency live today.
- **A3.** Every public number is a percentage with no baseline, and one of them is a range. **RF-05**, and **RF-18** if the return's shift-fill figure differs from the "40%+" and "20–40%" already in print.
- **C6.** Nothing public on uptime, SLA or security attestation. **RF-07** as written if the return has no figure and no commitment. Ask about the StatusGator record, and let them say whether it is theirs.
- **E2.** Privately held, founder-chairman, recent liquidity from the Sandata sale, no venture investors to answer to. An equity or co-development conversation is structurally simple here, which is unusual in this field. Watch **RF-12**: the natural deflection is to offer CareCommunity or "performance-based pricing" as the partnership.
- **Who wrote this.** The named leadership is CEO, COO, CFO and a GM, all from sales, commercial operations, finance and an EMR vendor. If the return is fluent in aide staffing and thin on visits, episodes and disciplines, that is the company, not the author.

## Durability read — evidence only

- Age eight years; same market throughout; three names for the product in seven years.
- Privately held by a founder who sold his main company, Sandata, to HHAeXchange in September 2024. No outside capital found, so no runway pressure visible and no valuation to defend; also no public financials.
- About 92 staff across three continents; whole executive team replaced or added between Aug 2023 and Jan 2025, all from IntelyCare, AlayaCare or outside healthcare.
- Revenue growth of 80% reported for 2023; base not given; nothing since.
- Business is home care aides, concentrated in New York Medicaid; the release says "all fifty states" but every named relationship is New York, Pennsylvania or the Carolinas.
- Product scope is expanding faster than the customer list is being published: seven new modules or partnerships in eighteen months, no new named customer in that period.
- Named integrations are with the founder's own companies (Sandata, Mobile Health) or with EVV platforms; HHAeXchange now owns Sandata and lists CareConnect as a partner.
- Our shape against theirs: three thousand skilled clinicians on episodic visit schedules, against a product built for hourly aide shifts. We would be a different kind of customer, not just a bigger one.

## Questions the research raises

For the demo or the reference call. One line each.

1. Name one Medicare-certified home health agency live on the platform today, and how many clinicians it schedules.
2. What is the scheduling unit in your data model: a shift on a case, or a visit in an episode with a discipline and a frequency?
3. For Sandata and HHAeXchange, what data actually moves, which direction, and how often? Is it more than single sign-on?
4. What is the Axxess integration, technically, and who is live on it?
5. Have you ever integrated with a system that has no public API? How?
6. Who leads engineering and product, and where do they sit?
7. Was CareConnect part of the Sandata sale to HHAeXchange? What is the relationship today?
8. What share of revenue is New York Medicaid personal care, and what happened to it through the 2025 CDPAP transition?
9. Give the period, baseline and agency count behind "40% more shifts". Is it 40% or 20–40%?
10. Is the StatusGator "CareConnect" status page yours? What was uptime over the last twelve months?
11. How does Caregiver Choice behave when the clinician builds their own week and the office only fills gaps?
12. How many caregivers and how many agencies are on the platform today?

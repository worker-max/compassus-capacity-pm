## One paragraph

Zeeva is a single web page with a waitlist form. It proposes a marketplace in which home health
clinicians — the discipline picker offers RN, LVN/LPN, PT, PTA, OT, COTA, SLP, MSW — browse
individual visits offered by **multiple agencies**, pick the ones that suit their location, skills,
pay rate and schedule, arrange the time directly with the patient, document with AI help, and get
paid quickly. The footer says *© Zeeva Connect, Inc.* and *Built by healthcare operators & AI
engineers*. No person, place, date, investor, customer, agency, integration, price or policy is
named. The sitemap was last touched in August 2025. Everything material about this company is
unknown, and one thing about it is unusually clear: **its customer is the clinician, and the visits
it fills come from agencies like ours.**

## The thing this dossier exists to say

Every other vendor on this roster sells to the agency. **Zeeva sells to the clinician**, and its core
promise to that clinician is: work for many agencies at once, at market rates, without applying to
any of them, and pick only the visits you want.

Read that as a capacity proposition and it is genuinely interesting — a way to reach clinician
supply that does not sit on our payroll, which is one real answer to a branch that cannot staff a
Friday. Read it as a labour-market proposition and it is something else: **a mechanism that puts
our own clinicians' marginal hours out to competitive bid**, prices them against other agencies'
visits, and removes the scheduler from the middle of the relationship between clinician, patient and
visit. And a model where the clinician “coordinate[s] visit times directly with patients” dissolves
the thing our whole initiative is trying to make measurable: who is available, for what, when.

Both readings can be true. Neither is a score. But the demo question is not the usual one, and
should be asked first: **are you selling us access to clinicians, or selling our clinicians access
to everyone else's visits?**

## Durability read — evidence only

There is nothing to weigh. No funding, no headcount, no customers, no shipped product, no legal
address, no named human, no policy documents, and a public footprint about thirteen months old whose
only page has not been rebuilt since. A marketplace is additionally the hardest business model to
start, because it needs clinician supply and agency demand at the same time in the same postcode,
and no evidence of either side was found. **The durability question cannot be answered from public
sources. That is itself the answer for now.**

## Questions the research raises

1. Who runs this company, where is it, when was it founded, and how many people work there?
2. Is anything live? How many clinicians are on the waitlist, and how many visits have been
   completed through the platform?
3. Which agencies supply visits today, by name?
4. **Are you selling to agencies or to clinicians, and what happens when both are our people?**
5. Are you funded? By whom, how much, and what is the runway?
6. Why is there no privacy policy or terms of service on a platform that would hold PHI, clinician
   licences and payment data?
7. Who employs the clinician on a Zeeva visit — the agency, Zeeva, or nobody? Who carries the
   liability, the licensure verification, the background check and the workers' compensation?
8. A visit documented in Zeeva has to land in the agency's system of record for billing, OASIS and
   survey. How? Which system, by what mechanism?
9. What is the model provider behind “AI assistance”, and does PHI reach it?
10. How does Zeeva make money — a take rate on the visit, an agency subscription, or a clinician fee?
11. “Market-driven pay rates”: who sets the rate, the agency or the market?

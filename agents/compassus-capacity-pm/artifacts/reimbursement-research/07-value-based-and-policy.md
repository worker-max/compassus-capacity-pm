# Value-Based Payment, Quality-Linked Revenue, and In-Flight Policy in Medicare Home Health

**Research date: 2026-08-18.** Every claim below carries an inline source URL and a date. Finalized policy and proposed policy are labeled separately throughout. Section 5 ("Confirmed as of 2026-08-18") is the freshness-critical block covering in-flight items.

**One correction up front, because it changes the product framing:** the claims-based *Acute Care Hospitalization (ACH)* and *ED Use Without Hospitalization* measures were **retired from HHVBP effective the CY2025 performance year**. They were replaced by the within-stay **Potentially Preventable Hospitalization (PPH)** measure and **Discharge to Community – Post-Acute Care (DTC-PAC)**. ED use is no longer scored in HHVBP at all. Any product messaging built on "we lower your ACH and ED-use HHVBP scores" is describing a measure set that no longer exists. (Source: CMS Expanded HHVBP FAQs, June 2026, Q3010.1 — https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-faqs)

### 1.3 How the Total Performance Score works

- **Care points** are earned per measure: **the higher of achievement points or improvement points**. Achievement runs 0–10 points; improvement runs 0–9 points. Max possible care points per measure = 10.
- **Achievement Score = 10 × (HHA performance score − achievement threshold) ÷ (benchmark − achievement threshold)**, rounded to three decimals.
- **Improvement Score = 9 × (HHA performance score − HHA improvement threshold) ÷ (benchmark − HHA improvement threshold)**, where the improvement threshold is the agency's own baseline-year score.
- **Achievement threshold** = the **median (50th percentile)** of all Medicare-certified HHAs' scores on that measure in the Model baseline year, set separately per cohort.
- **Benchmark** = the **mean of the top decile (90th percentile)** in the Model baseline year, set separately per cohort. Used in both achievement and improvement math.
- **Weighted measure points = (care points ÷ 10) × measure weight.** TPS = sum of weighted measure points, on a 0–100 scale.
- If measures or whole categories are missing, weights are **redistributed proportionally** so the remaining weights sum to 100.

Sources: CMS Expanded HHVBP FAQs, June 2026, Exhibit 3 glossary (Achievement Score, Achievement Threshold, Benchmark, Improvement Score, Care Points), https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-faqs; CMS "How Care Points Become the Total Performance Score (TPS)," https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-care-points-tps-written-resource.

Because achievement is scored against a **fixed baseline-year median and 90th percentile**, and improvement against the agency's own baseline, HHVBP is *not* a pure zero-sum tournament at the scoring layer. It becomes zero-sum only at the payment layer.

### 1.4 Data sufficiency — the small-branch cliff

| Measure type | Minimum for a score |
|---|---|
| OASIS-based measures | **20 completed home health quality episodes** per reporting period |
| Claims-based measures | **20 home health stays** per reporting period |
| HHCAHPS measures | **40 completed surveys** per reporting period (larger-volume cohort only) |
| To receive a TPS at all | Sufficient data for **at least 5 of the 12 applicable measures** in both baseline and performance years |

Source: CMS Expanded HHVBP FAQs, June 2026, Q3004 and Q4001.2, https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-faqs.

An agency that misses these floors receives IPRs but **no TPS and no payment adjustment**. For a multi-branch operator this matters: HHVBP is scored at the **CCN level**, not the branch level, so branch-level scheduling behavior aggregates into one CCN score.

### 1.5 From TPS to dollars — the linear exchange function

The **adjusted payment percentage (APP)** is computed per cohort via a linear exchange function (LEF), in seven steps, and is **budget-neutral within cohort**:

1. Prior Year Payment = each HHA's total Medicare FFS home health payments for the year before the payment year.
2. Unadjusted Payment Amount = 5% × Prior Year Payment.
3. TPS-Adjusted Payment Amount = (TPS ÷ 100) × Unadjusted Payment Amount.
4. **LEF ratio = Σ(Unadjusted Payment Amounts) ÷ Σ(TPS-Adjusted Payment Amounts)** across the cohort.
5–7. The LEF ratio is applied to each HHA's TPS to produce its APP, capped at ±5%.

Source: CMS "How the Total Performance Score (TPS) Becomes the Final Payment Adjustment," https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-tps-final-pay-adj-written-resource. In CMS's worked example the LEF ratio was 1.931, meaning an agency needed a TPS near 52 just to break even.

**Practical read:** the break-even TPS is set by the cohort's payment-weighted average TPS, not by any fixed number. Improving in absolute terms while the cohort improves faster still produces a negative adjustment. Appeals that change one agency's score force CMS to recalculate every agency's APP in that cohort to preserve budget neutrality (FAQ, June 2026, §V).

### 1.6 How a scheduling decision actually moves an HHVBP measure

**PPH — Within-Stay Potentially Preventable Hospitalization (15% of TPS, larger-volume; also 1 of 7 star measures).**
Risk-adjusted, claims-based, and — critically — it includes home health stays with an **end date** during the 12-month performance period, not a start date. That is a change from the retired ACH/ED-Use measures, which used start dates. Source: CMS Expanded HHVBP FAQs, June 2026, Q3027.1, https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/media/document/hhvbp-exp-faqs. Operationally that means the measure window closes when the *stay* closes, so a long stay drags exposure across quarters and a late discharge shifts a case into a different measurement year.

The scheduling levers that move PPH:
- **Time-to-first-visit after referral.** The whole HHVBP theory of change is catching deterioration before it becomes an ED visit; CMS's own model description says the model exists to "address health issues before they require an emergency room visit" (CMS Expanded HHVBP Model page, highlights).
- **Front-loading in the first 7–14 days**, when readmission hazard is highest. This is a scheduling-density decision, not a clinical-protocol decision — the protocol is worthless if the visit slots don't exist.
- **Missed and rescheduled visits** in the high-risk window. A skipped Friday nursing visit on an unstable CHF patient is a Sunday ED visit that lands in the numerator.
- **After-hours and weekend coverage.** PPH counts hospitalizations occurring *during* the stay, including on days no one is scheduled.
- **Clinician continuity.** Fewer distinct clinicians per patient means deterioration is recognized against a known baseline.
- **Discipline mix and sequencing** — nursing for medication reconciliation (the largest preventable-admission driver), PT for fall risk.

**OASIS functional measures (Discharge Function Score 15%; Bathing 3.5%; Upper Body Dressing 1.75%; Lower Body Dressing 1.75%; plus Dyspnea 7% and Oral Meds 11% — 40% of TPS in total).**
These are computed from **paired start/resumption-of-care and end-of-care OASIS assessments**, with the quality episode counted in the period in which it *ends*. Source: CMS Home Health Star Ratings page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health/home-health-star-ratings (accessed 2026-08-18).

Scheduling levers:
- **Therapy dose and cadence before discharge.** Functional improvement is dose-responsive; the schedule is the dose. MedPAC reports in-person visits per full 30-day period fell from 10.2 in 2019 to **8.4 in 2024**, with PT/OT/SLP down 21.5% over that span (MedPAC March 2026 Report, Chapter 8, Table 8-4, https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar26_Ch8_MedPAC_Report_To_Congress_SEC.pdf). Visit compression is directly eroding the input to 40% of the TPS.
- **Completing the discharge OASIS at all, and on time.** An episode that ends in transfer or death, or that never gets a paired end-of-care assessment, produces no improvement measurement — and pushes the agency toward the 20-episode floor.
- **Who performs the discharge assessment.** A clinician who has not seen the patient recently will score function inconsistently with the SOC assessment, which manufactures artificial non-improvement.
- **LUPA interaction.** LUPA thresholds range from **two to five in-person visits** depending on the PDGM payment group (MedPAC March 2026, Ch. 8, Table 8-4 note, same URL). A period that drops to LUPA is simultaneously a revenue event *and* an under-dosed functional-improvement episode. One missed visit can do both.

**MSPB-PAC (10%).** Total Medicare spending during the HH stay plus 30 days after. Home health visit scheduling barely moves the numerator directly; hospitalizations, SNF admissions, and readmissions do. It is effectively a second, wider-aperture bet on the same avoided-hospitalization behavior as PPH.

**HHCAHPS (20%, larger-volume only — Overall Rating and Willingness to Recommend).** Both surviving measures are global-judgment items. The revised survey retains a question on **whether staff kept the patient informed about when they would arrive** (Communication composite, Q15 in the original; retained in the revised composite) — a literal scheduling-reliability item. Source: CMS HHCAHPS page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health-quality-reporting-program/home-health-care-cahpsr-hhcahps-survey (page last modified 2026-04-20). Visit punctuality and schedule-change communication are the most direct operational input to the two HHCAHPS measures that still carry 20% of the TPS.

## 2. Home Health Quality Reporting Program (HH QRP)

### 2.1 Pay-for-reporting penalty (FINALIZED, longstanding)

Section 1895(b)(3)(B)(v) of the Social Security Act requires HHAs to submit CMS-specified HH QRP data; **noncompliance costs 2 percentage points off the annual payment update (APU)**. HHAs must hit a **90% OASIS submission compliance rate for all submitted OASIS data regardless of payer**, and must meet the HHCAHPS participation requirement. Sources: CMS HH QRP Reconsideration page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health/home-health-quality-reporting-reconsideration-and-exception-extension; LeadingAge, "CMS Posts Home Health CY 2026 APU Non-Compliance Letters," https://leadingage.org/cms-posts-home-health-cy-2026-apu-non-compliance-letters/.

HHCAHPS participation is required for agencies serving **60 or more** unduplicated survey-eligible patients in the 12-month reference period; agencies with **59 or fewer** may file a Participation Exemption Request. The CY2028 APU is driven by April 2026–March 2027 data collection. Source: CMS HHCAHPS page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health-quality-reporting-program/home-health-care-cahpsr-hhcahps-survey (2026-04-20).

The CY2026 final rule added a **reconsideration pathway** allowing HHAs to request reconsideration of a noncompliance determination, plus a limited extraordinary-circumstances extension (natural or man-made disaster including cyberattack) during the 30-day reconsideration window. Source: CY2026 final rule fact sheet (2025-11-28).

## 3. Star Ratings

### 3.1 Quality of Patient Care (QoPC) Star Rating — FINALIZED methodology

**Seven measures** (unchanged in 2026):

1. Timely Initiation of Care (process)
2. Improvement in Ambulation
3. Improvement in Bed Transferring
4. Improvement in Bathing
5. Improvement in Shortness of Breath (Dyspnea)
6. Improvement in Management of Oral Medications
7. **Home Health Within-Stay Potentially Preventable Hospitalization (PPH)** — replaced Acute Care Hospitalization effective **May 2024**

Sources: CMS Home Health Star Ratings page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health/home-health-star-ratings; CMS "Home Health Quality of Patient Care Star Rating Provider Preview Report" (July 2026 refresh sample), https://www.cms.gov/files/document/hh-quality-patient-care-star-rating-july2026-sample-provider-preview-pdf.pdf.

**Computation (July 2026 refresh sample report, same URL):**
1. For each of the 7 measures, sort all HHAs low→high and split into **10 roughly equal groups (deciles)**.
2. Assign an initial rating of **0.5 to 5.0 in 0.5 increments** by decile.
3. **Adjust toward the middle**: a one-sided binomial significance test at **p = 0.05** against the national middle categories (2.5 and 3.0). If the agency's score is not statistically different from the middle, its rating moves 0.5 toward the middle.
4. Average the adjusted ratings across up to 7 measures, round to nearest 0.5.
5. Map the rounded average to a final star from **1.0 to 5.0 in half-star increments** (9 categories; 3.0 is the middle). Note the mapping compresses: an average of 4.5 *or* 5.0 both yield 5.0 stars.

**Thresholds:** 20 completed quality episodes (OASIS measures) or 20 home health stays (claims measures) per measure to report that measure; **at least 5 of 7 measures** to get a star rating at all. Same sources.

**Refresh cadence:** **quarterly** (January, April, July, October). Provider Preview reports arrive roughly **3.5 months** before public posting. Source: CMS Home Health Star Ratings page.

**Data lag, July 2026 refresh (concrete):** OASIS-based measures use end-of-care assessments from **10/1/2024–9/30/2025**; the PPH claims measure uses **1/1/2024–12/31/2024** claims. Source: July 2026 Provider Preview sample, https://www.cms.gov/files/document/hh-quality-patient-care-star-rating-july2026-sample-provider-preview-pdf.pdf. **The claims measure in today's public star rating is roughly 19–31 months old.** That is a hard constraint on any "improve your star rating" ROI story.

### 3.2 Patient Survey (HHCAHPS) Star Rating — FINALIZED

Based on **four** of the publicly reported HHCAHPS measures: Care of Patients, Communication Between Providers and Patients, Specific Care Issues, and Overall Rating of Care. **Willingness to Recommend is excluded** from the star. Source: CMS Home Health Star Ratings page, https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/home-health/home-health-star-ratings.

**Threshold: 40 or more completed surveys** over the four-quarter reporting period. Below that, HHCAHPS data are still publicly reported but no star is issued. **Refresh: quarterly.** Provider Preview reports arrive about **one month** before posting. Same source.

**2026 disruption:** the survey was revised effective the **April 2026 sample month**, and the Specific Care Issues composite was broken into three standalone measures. Since Specific Care Issues is one of the four star inputs, the Patient Survey star composition necessarily changes as the four-quarter window rolls over to revised-instrument data. **I could not confirm from a CMS source what the post-transition Patient Survey star measure set will be, nor whether CMS will pause or trend-break the star during the transition.** The CMS Home Health Star Ratings page as of 2026-08-18 still lists the pre-revision four measures with the old question numbers. Treat this as an open question and check https://homehealthcahps.org/ for the current technical notes.

### 3.3 Commercial effect on referral volume — the honest evidence

This is where the industry narrative and the published evidence diverge, and the divergence is worth knowing before it appears in a sales deck.

**Evidence that stars move patient choice — but only slightly:**
Schwartz et al., *Health Services Research*, February 2022 (PMID 34390253, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34390253/). Conditional-logit discrete-choice model on 186,498 new Medicare FFS home health admissions, 2014–2016. Introducing star ratings was associated with a **0.88 percentage-point increase** in the probability of selecting a high-quality-star HHA and a **0.81 percentage-point increase** for a high patient-experience-star HHA. Effects were larger for community-admitted, Black, and dual-eligible beneficiaries. The authors themselves note the relationships were **"weaker than observed in other health care settings."**

**Evidence that stars do *not* move market share:**
Jun Li, *Medical Care Research and Review*, April 2024 (PMID 38235538, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38235538/). Natural experiment. Higher-rated agencies increased market share by **1.4%, or 0.25 percentage points (95% CI −0.63 to 1.12)** — described by the author as "a practically and statistically insignificant amount," with **precise null effects** even among community-entry patients and patients in competitive markets. Conclusion: "The star ratings are unlikely to improve home health quality despite continued policymaker interest."

**Evidence that the ratings are nonetheless clinically valid:**
Jun Li, *Medical Care*, 2024 (PMID 37796230, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37796230/). Instrumental-variables study of 1,870,080 Medicare FFS home health beneficiaries. Treatment by the highest-rated available agency reduced hospitalization risk by **3.2 percentage points** (95% CI −4.1 to −2.3), ED use by **2.2 points** (−3.2 to −1.1), and institutionalization by **0.9 points** (−1.3 to −0.5), and increased days independently at home by **3.75 days** over 180 days post-episode (**6.51 days** for above-average agencies; **7.80 days** where the top agency was ≥1 star above the next best).

**Net read for a capacity platform:** star ratings are a *valid* signal of outcomes but a *weak* driver of consumer-side referral volume in the published literature. The commercially meaningful channel is institutional, not consumer — hospital preferred-network selection, ACO narrowing, MA plan network decisions — and I **could not find a peer-reviewed quantification of the star-rating → hospital-preferred-network → volume link**. Do not assert a referral-volume ROI number you cannot source.

**National QoPC star distribution:** CMS states that "most agencies fall 'in the middle' with 3 stars" (July 2026 Provider Preview sample, https://www.cms.gov/files/document/hh-quality-patient-care-star-rating-july2026-sample-provider-preview-pdf.pdf). **I could not confirm a published current count of agencies at each star level as of 2026-08-18.** The underlying data are downloadable from the Care Compare provider data files if an exact distribution is needed.

## 4. How MA and commercial contracts use quality

### 4.1 Market context (confirmed)

- MedPAC: in 2024, about **2.7 million** FFS Medicare beneficiaries received home health, Medicare spent **$16.0 billion**, and **over 12,000 HHAs** were certified. FFS Medicare margin for freestanding HHAs was **21.2%** in 2024; all-payer margin was **5.0%**. Source: MedPAC March 2026 Report to Congress, Chapter 8, https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar26_Ch8_MedPAC_Report_To_Congress_SEC.pdf (2026-03-12). The 21.2% FFS margin against a 5.0% all-payer margin is the cleanest available proxy for how much worse non-FFS (largely MA) business performs.
- MedPAC on MA mix: "Typically, HHAs serve a mix of Medicare Advantage (MA) and FFS Medicare beneficiaries, reflecting local enrollment patterns." Same source.
- Industry estimate (**not authoritative**): MA pays roughly **70–85% of FFS rates per equivalent episode**, with MA penetration above ~50% of an agency's mix materially compressing per-episode revenue. Attributed to Hendon Partners, "Medicare Advantage Impact on Home Health Valuation 2026," dated 2026-05-04 (surfaced via search; the direct URL returned 404 on 2026-08-18, so treat the figure as directional M&A-advisory commentary, not a verified benchmark).

### 4.2 What the 2026 MA rules actually changed (FINALIZED)

The CY2026 Medicare Advantage final rule (released 2025-04-11):
- **"Organization determinations" now include concurrent review decisions**, and plans **may no longer retroactively deny approved services** except for fraud or under limited CMS reopening criteria. This prevents mid-episode service terminations for home health patients.
- Plans must **notify providers directly** of coverage-request outcomes when the provider submitted the request.
- Appeals were improved specifically for **termination of coverage for home health or post-acute care services**.
- MA plans must conduct **annual health-equity analyses of utilization-management policies**.
Source: Home Care Association of Florida, "What the 2026 Medicare Advantage Final Rule Means for Home Health Providers," https://members.homecarefla.org/provider-updates/Details/what-the-2026-medicare-advantage-final-rule-means-for-home-health-providers-261475 (2025-04-11).

### 4.3 The direction of travel is *away* from MA value-based upside

This is the most important and most counter-narrative finding in this section.

At Home Health Care News' June 2026 PAYER Summit, **Devin Woodley, VP of managed care contracting at VNS Health**, said: *"When it comes to Medicare Advantage and managed Medicaid, we've actually seen the opposite"* of CMS-driven innovation — noting quality measures being *reduced* in some managed markets, which "decreas[es] the opportunity to do better rates," and that payer insiders at large plans "actually don't want to share in the pie." His summary: large national MA and managed Medicaid plans "are just trying to stay afloat," which means **fewer value-based opportunities for providers**. Source: Home Health Care News, "Inside 3 Payment Trends Shaping Home-Based Care," https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/06/inside-3-payment-trends-shaping-home-based-care/ (2026-06-25).

At the same event, **Allison Rizer (ATI Advisory)** argued the definition of value is shifting from cost savings to quality: *"Are we at least getting the same or higher quality for what we're spending?"* And **Arun Dahiya of OneHome** (a Humana affiliate acting as a payer-neutral post-acute convener) said site-of-care decisions follow from a provider's receptiveness to value-based models — i.e. **VBC participation is itself a referral-steering criterion**. Same source.

### 4.4 Prior authorization is now the dominant MA-quality interaction

- An **HHS Office of Inspector General investigation** (reported June 2026) found the **three largest MA organizations denied post-acute services at higher rates than smaller peers**. MAOs collectively **overturned 36% of long-term care hospital denials and 43% of appealed inpatient rehabilitation facility denials**; IRF overturn rates ranged from **14% to 86%** by plan. OIG recommended CMS collect request-level prior-authorization data by service type and contractor. Source: Home Health Care News, "What Upstream Prior Authorization Denials Mean for Home Health Providers," https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/06/what-upstream-prior-authorization-denials-mean-for-home-health-providers/ (2026-06-18).
- **The capacity-platform implication is direct.** When an MA plan denies IRF or LTCH placement, the hospital still has to discharge the patient. Home health absorbs **higher-acuity patients on compressed, short-notice timelines** — HHCN: "home health providers may have to start care even sooner than is normally expected and coordinate services on short notice… in an industry that is chronically struggling with staffing." Same source. This is arguably the single strongest demand-side argument for a branch capacity-and-scheduling product in 2026.
- **More than half** of respondents to an HHCN survey named Medicare Advantage the payer type exerting the most pressure on their organization, citing administrative burden, prior-authorization delays, and denials alongside rates. Some providers are **limiting admissions for certain payers**. Source: Home Health Care News, "Medicare Advantage, Medicaid Persist As Top Concern Among Home-Based Care Leaders," https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/06/... (2026-06-14; article listed in HHCN's Medicare Advantage archive, https://homehealthcarenews.com/category/medicare-advantage).

### 4.5 What I could NOT confirm

I was unable, within this session's search budget, to verify **named, published home health value-based contracts** with specific quality gates — e.g. a named MA plan tying HH rates or network status to a stated readmission threshold or star-rating floor, or disclosed percentages of MA volume under episodic/shared-savings arrangements in Enhabit, Amedisys, Aveanna, BrightSpring, Pennant, or Addus earnings materials. **Treat the existence of such contracts as industry-reported but unverified here.** The verified 2026 signal points the other way: MA plans are narrowing, not expanding, value-based upside for home health. Anyone building a business case on MA shared-savings should validate against primary earnings-call transcripts before relying on it.

Confirmed adjacent facts worth noting: Enhabit was acquired by private equity firm **Kinderhook Industries**, with **Dale Clift** named CEO in May 2026 (Home Health Care News, https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/05/enhabits-new-ceo-aims-to-send-company-into-a-chapter-of-growth/, 2026-05-26). **Choice Health at Home** is pursuing an explicit "matching the market" MA strategy plus a hub-and-spoke density model (HHCN, 2026-08-03, https://homehealthcarenews.com/category/medicare-advantage) — a density strategy is a scheduling-efficiency strategy.

**IN FLIGHT — Medicare Home Health Payment Integrity and Protection Act of 2026 (S. 5250).**
- Introduced **August 7, 2026** by Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) and Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN).

- Would **direct CMS to reset home health payment rates** to adjust for fraud in the data and for post-COVID utilization change — i.e. undo the compounding cuts — plus: fingerprinting of administrators at agencies deemed extreme fraud risk; proof of liability insurance for those agencies; more frequent surveys for newly enrolled agencies, ownership changes, and reactivated billing privileges; **increased financial penalties for failing to report quality data**; increased inspections for agencies that fail to submit quality data; CMS surveyor training requirements; law-enforcement funding.

### 5.3 Review Choice Demonstration (RCD) — no expansion confirmed

- **States: Illinois, Ohio, Texas, North Carolina, Florida, Oklahoma.** All in Palmetto GBA's JM jurisdiction.
- Extended for **five additional years effective June 1, 2024** (i.e. through approximately May 31, 2029).
- **Choice 3 (Minimal Review with 25% Payment Reduction) was removed** as an initial choice in that extension; initial options are now **pre-claim review** or **postpayment review**. Providers who make no selection default to postpayment review.
- Relief from most review requires a **90% affirmation/approval rate on a minimum of 10 requests/claims**.
- CMS retains authority to expand to additional JM-jurisdiction states on evidence of fraud, waste, or abuse.
- **Status check:** the CMS RCD page's last substantive timeline update is 09/16/2025 (FY24 statistics). **Page Last Modified: 07/14/2026.** **No new state expansion has been announced. I explicitly could not confirm any 2026 RCD expansion**, despite industry advocacy (e.g. AccentCare's CY2026 comment letter urging expansion to California and nationwide).
- Source: https://www.cms.gov/data-research/monitoring-programs/medicare-fee-service-compliance-programs/prior-authorization-and-pre-claim-review-initiatives/review-choice-demonstration-home-health-services (accessed 2026-08-18).

### 5.4 WISeR Model — **does not touch home health**

- Launched **January 1, 2026**; runs six performance years through **December 31, 2031**.
- **Six states: New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, Arizona, Washington.**
- Participants are technology companies, not providers: Cohere Health (TX), Genzeon (NJ), Humata Health (OK), Innovaccer (OH), Virtix Health (WA), Zyter (AZ).
- **Selected services are skin and tissue substitutes, implantation of electrical nerve stimulators, and knee arthroscopy for knee osteoarthritis.** The model **excludes inpatient-only services, emergency services, and services that would pose substantial risk if delayed**. It does not apply to Medicare Advantage.
- All non-payment recommendations must be made by appropriately licensed clinicians. A **"gold carding" exemption** for compliance-demonstrated providers is described as a future feature.
- **Explicit finding: home health, and post-acute care generally, is NOT within the WISeR service list as published.** Anyone claiming WISeR is an AI prior-authorization threat to home health is wrong as of 2026-08-18.
- Source: https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/wiser (accessed 2026-08-18).

### 5.5 MA prior authorization — voluntary pledge and the CMS-0057 deadline

**Voluntary industry pledge (AHIP / BCBSA, announced June 2025):** plans covering nearly **270 million Americans** across Commercial, Medicare Advantage, and Medicaid managed care. Commitments **already in effect since January 1, 2026**:
- Specific reductions in the scope of claims subject to prior authorization, set plan by plan;
- **Honoring existing prior authorizations for benefit-equivalent in-network services for a 90-day transition** when a patient changes plans;
- Clear explanations of determinations with appeal support;
- Medical-professional review of all clinically based non-approvals.

**2027 commitments:** at least **80% of electronic prior-authorization approvals answered in real time** (with complete clinical documentation), and a standardized FHIR-based electronic PA framework **operational by January 1, 2027**.
Source: AHIP, "2026 Will Bring Progress on Simplifying Prior Authorization," https://www.ahip.org/news/articles/2026-will-bring-progress-on-simplifying-prior-authorization (2026-01-07).

**Regulatory backstop (FINALIZED rule, CMS-0057-F):** certain CMS-regulated payers — including Medicare Advantage — **must implement and maintain the Patient Access, Provider Directory, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization APIs starting January 1, 2027.** CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz reiterated the January 1, 2027 deadline in a **May 2026** announcement, and on **May 15, 2026** CMS announced a coalition of ~29 health systems, EHR vendors (Epic, Oracle, athenahealth, ModMed, eClinicalWorks, MEDITECH) and data networks to implement the APIs in real workflows. Sources: CMS Electronic Prior Authorization page, https://www.cms.gov/priorities/electronic-prior-authorization (page last modified 2026-08-04); Home Health Care News, "Dr. Oz Outlines Prior Authorization Plans: What It Means For Home Health," https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/05/dr-oz-outlines-prior-authorization-plans-what-it-means-for-home-health/ (2026-05-06).

Separately, CMS's **CY2027 Physician Fee Schedule proposed rule** proposes updates to the MIPS Promoting Interoperability electronic prior authorization measure, and the **FY2027 IPPS final rule** made the Electronic Prior Authorization measure an **optional bonus measure for CY2027 and mandatory beginning CY2028** for eligible hospitals and CAHs. Source: CMS Electronic Prior Authorization page, same URL.

**Caveat:** none of the above obligates MA plans to reduce prior authorization on *home health* specifically, and the OIG findings in §4.4 indicate post-acute denials remain elevated at the largest MAOs as of mid-2026.

### 5.6 Other 2026 home-health-specific CMS actions

**National Medicare enrollment moratorium on home health and hospice — FINALIZED and in effect.** Announced **May 2026**, effective immediately, for **six months**, extendable in six-month increments. Applies to **all initial Medicare enrollment applications and certain changes in majority ownership**. Existing enrollments are unaffected. CMS Administrator Dr. Oz framed it as "shutting the door on fraud." The Alliance objected that moratoria do not distinguish bad actors from compliant providers and will reduce competition and rural access. A parallel DMEPOS moratorium was also in place — three moratoria total. Source: Home Health Care News, "[Updated] CMS Places National Moratorium On Home Health, Hospice Agency Enrollment," https://homehealthcarenews.com/2026/05/cms-places-national-moratorium-on-home-health-hospice-agency-enrollment/ (2026-05).

**Direct implication for a branch capacity platform: you cannot solve a capacity shortfall by standing up a new CCN right now.** Growth has to come from throughput at existing CCNs — which is exactly the value proposition — or from acquisition, which is also constrained by the change-of-majority-ownership piece of the moratorium.

**CY2026 face-to-face encounter change (FINALIZED).** 42 CFR 424.22(a)(1)(v) was broadened so that **physicians, in addition to NPs, CNSs, and PAs**, may perform the F2F encounter regardless of whether they are the certifying practitioner or cared for the patient in the acute/post-acute facility. Source: CY2026 final rule fact sheet (2025-11-28).

**CY2026 provider-enrollment tightening (FINALIZED):** more grounds for retroactive revocation; deactivation of physicians/practitioners who have not ordered or certified services for 12 consecutive months; reaffirmed revocation authority where beneficiaries attest services were not furnished. Same source.

## 6. Hospital-at-home and post-acute competition

**Acute Hospital Care at Home (AHCAH) waiver — EXTENDED, statutory, long runway.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Current statutory end date | **September 30, 2030** |
| Extended by | **Section 6210 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 — Public Law 119-75, enacted February 3, 2026** |
| Prior extension chain | P.L. 119-37 (2025-11-12) → Jan 30, 2026; P.L. 119-4 (2025-03-15) → Sep 30, 2025; P.L. 118-158 (2024-12-21) → Mar 31, 2025 |
| New waiver requests | CMS stops accepting after **September 1, 2030** |
| Required study | Subsequent study due by **September 30, 2029** |

Sources: 42 U.S.C. §1395cc-7 with amendment notes, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:42%20section:1395cc-7%20edition:prelim) (accessed 2026-08-18); CMS QualityNet AHCAH page, https://qualitynet.cms.gov/acute-hospital-care-at-home (accessed 2026-08-18).

**Waiver mechanics that matter operationally:** waivers are granted per **CMS Certification Number**, not per health system — a seven-hospital system needs a separate request for each participating hospital. Hospitals with **25 or more** prior at-home acute patients get an expedited attestation process and report monthly; hospitals with **fewer than 25** or no experience face a detailed process and report **weekly**. Source: QualityNet AHCAH page.

**What it means for home health branch volume.** The 2025 lapse is the useful evidence: the program lapsed on September 30, 2025 during the government shutdown, producing a **43-day disruption** for participating hospitals and patients before the November 2025 extension (reported at https://www.mindbowser.com/hospital-at-home-cms-waiver-guide/, 2026). Now that Congress has given the program a **four-and-a-half-year runway to 2030**, hospitals can finally capitalize AHCAH programs without annual cliff risk — which is precisely the condition under which volume scales.

For home health branches the effect is two-sided and I want to be careful not to overstate either side:
- **Substitution risk:** AHCAH patients are inpatients. Some share of them would otherwise have been discharged early to home health. Every AHCAH day is a day a home health agency is not the primary provider in that home.
- **Complementary demand:** AHCAH discharges still need post-acute follow-on, and many AHCAH programs subcontract the in-home nursing/paramedic visit component. Home health capacity in the same geography becomes a supply input to the hospital's program.

**I could not confirm a published estimate of AHCAH's displacement effect on home health episode volume**, and I did not find current 2026 participation counts (approved hospitals/systems) from CMS. Treat displacement magnitude as unquantified.

**Competing post-acute pressure, quantified:** MedPAC reports the share of general acute-care hospital discharges followed by at least one 30-day home health period was **18.0% in the first ten months of 2024**, down from a pandemic peak of 20.1% but still above the **15.8%** rate of 2019 — and **2.2 percentage points above the 2019 rate** for IPPS discharges specifically. Source: MedPAC March 2026, Ch. 8, https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar26_Ch8_MedPAC_Report_To_Congress_SEC.pdf.

Also relevant to substitution: MA denials of IRF/LTCH placement are pushing higher-acuity patients down the ladder toward SNF and home health (see §4.4).

## 7. Telehealth and virtual visits in home health

**The rule has not changed and is unambiguous. Telecommunications visits are not paid, do not count as visits, and do not count toward LUPA.**

Since **July 1, 2023**, HHAs must report telecommunications services on claims using three G-codes:

| Code | Service |
|---|---|
| **G0320** | Synchronous telemedicine, real-time two-way audio **and** video |
| **G0321** | Synchronous telemedicine, **audio-only** |
| **G0322** | Collection of physiologic data digitally transmitted (remote patient monitoring) |

These codes are **not considered a home visit** for purposes of:
- **Calculating LUPA add-on payments**
- **Outlier unit amounts**
- **Total visit counts**
- **Meeting covered skilled-visit requirements**

Source: CGS Medicare (HHH MAC), "Telehealth Home Health Services: New G-Codes," https://cgsmedicare.com/hhh/pubs/news/2022/12/cope133197.html.

Claims are returned if a G-code line item does not have another line item with the same revenue code and a G HCPCS code. Same source.

**No CY2026 or CY2027 change.** Neither the CY2026 final rule fact sheet (2025-11-28) nor the CY2027 proposed rule fact sheet (2026-07-01) contains any change to home health telecommunications payment, LUPA counting, or visit definition. Sources: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2026-home-health-prospective-payment-system-final-rule-cms-1828-f ; https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-home-health-prospective-payment-system-proposed-rule-fact-sheet-cms-1844-p.

**Do not conflate this with Part B telehealth flexibilities.** The Medicare telehealth flexibilities extended into 2027 are a **Part B physician-services** policy and have no bearing on how home health 30-day periods are paid. A **telehealth face-to-face encounter can satisfy the F2F certification requirement** (MedPAC March 2026, Ch. 8: "An encounter through telehealth services may satisfy the requirement") — that is the one place telehealth genuinely substitutes in the home health benefit.

**Actual utilization (MedPAC, 2024 claims):**
- **2.2% of 30-day periods** included a telehealth visit or remote patient monitoring.
- **About 12% of HHAs** reported at least one telehealth visit to an FFS beneficiary.
- **~375,000 telehealth visits** total in 2024; **~80%** were skilled nursing.
- Telehealth was **less than 1% of all visits**.
- MedPAC's conclusion: "most clinical care in the home health benefit is still provided in person."
Source: https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar26_Ch8_MedPAC_Report_To_Congress_SEC.pdf (2026-03-12).

**Product implication.** A scheduling platform cannot use virtual visits to fill LUPA gaps or to satisfy a payment threshold — that is a compliance trap. Virtual contact is legitimate as a *clinical surveillance* layer that reduces PPH (which is 15% of TPS and 1 of 7 star measures) and as a triage input to reallocate in-person capacity. It is not a billable substitute for a visit.

Where can this patient go?Six things any scheduling partner has to help us do.

Every day, new patients are referred to us. Taking them depends on one answer: who has room, where, and when? Today that answer is slow and hard to see. So we turn some patients away, say yes too late, or say yes and then struggle to staff the visits. Anything we buy has to help us serve more patients, faster, with the team we already have.

The standard we’re held to 48 hrs From referral to the first visit in the patient’s home. Across ~80 branches and ~3,000 clinicians.

How a patient reaches us, and where we lose them

Each step is a place where volume either gets through or leaks out.

The six pillars

The first one comes before everything else. For each pillar: what we need, what to show us, and the question we’ll ask.

I
Comes first

See our room

Know exactly how many more patients each branch can take, by type of clinician, this week and next. Today no one can answer that with confidence.

Show us
  • Open room by branch, area and type of clinician (nurse, therapist, aide)
  • Room that’s spoken for but waiting on insurer approval
  • New hires counted at the pace they can really carry, not full load
  • A forward view: next week, not just today
We’ll ask

“Using our real data, show us how many new patients one branch could take next week, and which clinicians would see them.”

We’ll measureVisits we could deliver vs. visits we do
II
More patients

Say yes faster

When a referral comes in, answer “where can this patient go, and when can we start?” in minutes, not hours. An open first-visit slot that isn’t filled today is gone for good.

Show us
  • Matches a new referral to a clinician with room, nearby, right away
  • Holds first-visit slots for new patients so routine visits don’t crowd them out
  • Offers a declined slot to the next patient quickly
We’ll ask

“A referral lands at 3pm on a Friday. Walk us through how your system gets that patient seen by Sunday.”

We’ll measureReferrals turned away for lack of roomHours from referral to first visit
III
More patients

Get insurance right the first time

Every insurer plays by different rules. Some approve visits in advance, some pay per care period, some cap visits per year. The rules exist, but they don’t reach the people planning care in time.

Show us
  • Each insurer’s rules visible when the care plan is built
  • Warnings before a visit falls outside what’s approved or covered
  • Where approvals are stuck, and with which insurer
We’ll ask

“How does your system know what each insurer has approved, and what does the scheduler see when a visit isn’t covered?”

We’ll measureVisits delivered but not paid · days waiting on approval
IV
More visits

Fill every visit

Patients cancel, clinicians call out, plans change. Every visit we don’t refill is care the patient doesn’t get, and pay the clinician doesn’t earn.

Show us
  • Cancelled visits refilled the same day, automatically offered to nearby clinicians
  • Aide schedules handled on their own track, separate from new-patient visits
  • Honest drive-time savings: 5–15% is realistic; claims of 30%+ aren’t
We’ll ask

“At a customer like us, how many more visits per clinician per day did they deliver, compared with branches that didn’t use it?”

We’ll measureMissed and rescheduled visits · overtime and agency staff
V
More time

Less chasing

Our coordinators spend their day on phone calls, alerts and manual hand-offs just to keep the schedule standing. That time should go to taking on patients.

Show us
  • Fewer, better alerts: only the ones that need action
  • Steps removed, not just done faster
  • Hand-offs between intake, scheduling and clinicians tracked in one place
We’ll ask

“Which of our daily tasks would your system eliminate entirely?”

We’ll measureHours from referral to each hand-off · coordinator time per new patient
VI
More capacity, kept

Steady weeks for clinicians

Most of our clinicians are paid per visit. An unpredictable week means unpredictable pay, and those clinicians leave. Every clinician we keep is room for more patients.

Show us
  • Keeps a clinician’s booked week full and predictable
  • Takes over the unpaid evening calls confirming tomorrow’s visits (~30 min a day)
  • Keeps patients with the same clinician wherever possible
We’ll ask

“How does your system give a clinician a steadier week, and how would we see that in their pay after 90 days?”

We’ll measureWeek-to-week swing in visits · clinician turnover

What we expect from any partner

These apply across all six pillars.

Our people decide

The system recommends and our people make the call. We’ve tried tools that took the decision away, and they didn’t stick.

“Which decisions does your system make on its own?”

Results, not activity

Show more patients served and more visits per clinician. “Percent of visits auto-scheduled” doesn’t tell us that.

“What changed for patients and clinicians at a customer like us?”

Works with what we have

We run Homecare Homebase (HCHB). You need to show exactly how you connect to it, and who carries that work.

“How does data move between you and HCHB, and how often?”

Priced in full

Three years, ~80 branches, including setup, training and connecting to our systems. Not just the license.

“What does year one cost us, all in?”

Real-world data

Our data isn’t perfect, and schedulers will override the system early on. It has to cope with both.

“What happens in the first 60 days when schedulers ignore your suggestions?”

Adds something new

We already own some scheduling tools. Show what you do that they don’t.

“What can you do that our current system can’t?”

When you quote us a number, tell us

Compared with what?Savings over our current tools, or over doing nothing at all.
Based on which patients?Medicare only, or every insurer we work with.
From where?A measured customer result, or a projection.
Over what time?When value starts, and when it reaches full effect.

How the three arenas connect

Priority key

  • GATE Hard or structural and a day-one must-have. A knockout — if it cannot be done, nothing downstream is trustworthy.
  • MVP Day-one must-have (workbook weight 3).
  • LATER Shapes the answer but not day one (weight 1).
  • CONFLICT A vendor's built-in way of working could contradict how Compassus already operates. Probe hardest here.

The envelope

Capacity Management

How much work this branch can deliver — people, hours, disciplines and territory, netted against what is already booked. SH-01–09 · C-01–13

  1. 01

    Staff supply by discipline & role

    GATE

    Headcount, discipline, assessing vs. assistant, FTE and employment type. The single biggest driver of what a branch can deliver — everything else works inside it.

    SH-01 SH-02 SH-03 SH-04 C-01

  2. 02

    Assessing capacity by discipline

    GATE

    Open room limited to clinicians who can perform a SOC or eval. This is the factor that gates saying yes to a referral — and the constraint the discovery names as the binding limit on branch growth.

    C-08 SH-03

  3. 03

    Availability & time off

    GATE

    Approved PTO and LOA blank the day out of capacity entirely. A hard blackout once entered — but the Workday↔HCHB feed is reported off, so this input runs stale today.

    SH-05

  4. 04

    Territory reachability

    GATECONFLICT

    Branch coverage by county and clinician assignment by zip. Room in the wrong area is room you cannot use. Census-tract granularity is under evaluation as a finer alternative.

    SH-06 C-02 C-03 C-04

  5. 05

    Per-diem & flex buffer

    GATE

    Contract and PRN capacity — the elastic cushion when full-time is exhausted. Stated availability is not real availability: actual engagement depends on the relationship.

    C-09

  6. 06

    On-call & weekend rotation load

    GATE

    Rotation duty consumes capacity and creates a recovery burden the weekday schedule has to absorb.

    C-12

  7. 07

    Visit weighting — the point currency

    MVPCONFLICT

    Routine 1.0, SOC 2.5, recert 1.75 and so on. The shared currency of both arenas: the same headcount delivers very different capacity depending on visit mix.

    SH-07

  8. 08

    Targets & ceiling

    MVPCONFLICT

    Defines what “full” means. Plan to the weekly target; the daily ceiling is the hard cap capacity cannot cross.

    SH-08

  9. 09

    Committed load & open room

    MVPCONFLICT

    Points already scheduled, and the bookable points left by day and across the week. The live read leaders act on — green means take more, red means full.

    C-05 C-06 C-07

  10. 10

    Specialty competency supply

    LATER

    Wound, IV, vent, lymphedema. Real capacity that only counts if the patient needs that skill — and it lives tacitly today, not credentialed in the data.

    C-10

  11. 11

    Orientation & ramp status

    LATER

    A new hire is headcount before they are capacity. Counting them at full productivity overstates the envelope.

    C-11

  12. —

    Referral volume

    OUT OF SCOPE

    Held steady by decision. Surfaced for context only, not modelled.

    C-13

Coordination

Everything performed against the envelope. Scheduling allocates the room; engagement defends it. The workbook names coordination “the execution glue that ties capacity and scheduling” — and the reason for the grouping is blunt: a schedule that is never confirmed, covered or rebooked is not delivered work.

Filling the envelope

Scheduling Engine

Assigning each ordered visit to a clinician, on a day, in a workable route. S-01–S-44 + 3 unnumbered

  1. 01

    Ordered demand

    GATE

    Plan-of-care orders, ordered frequency and visit type. Non-negotiable demand — it sets what must be scheduled and when, before any preference is considered.

    S-01 S-02

  2. 01½

    Authorization & add-on orders

    NEWGATE

    Two hard stops that sit upstream of the scheduler's queue, added to the inventory after the 13 Aug on-site. A referral must clear eligibility and have auth keyed before it is schedulable at all; plan-of-care QA must clear before the first week can be assigned. Most of the pain is self-inflicted — plans of care are written without regard to payer limits, and the payer rules already exist in a coordination note at verification. Surfacing them at plan-of-care creation is the highest-value, lowest-complexity win the session identified.

    Insurance Authorization · Add-On Orders — both Hard / MVP Yes / Assist, awaiting IDs

  3. 02

    Compliance windows

    GATE

    SOC timing, recert and face-to-face, the ordered-frequency window, and the supervisory-visit cadence. Regulatory clocks — fully clear, and the safest automation win on the board.

    S-35 S-36 S-03 S-37

  4. 03

    Assignment fit

    GATE

    Discipline and role match, specialty competency match, acuity to skill. Good fit uses capacity efficiently; assigning to whoever is free wastes it and strains clinicians. Acuity-to-skill stays a human call.

    S-15 S-16 S-33

  5. 03½

    Consent & POA readiness

    NEWGATE

    Consent has to be signed before the first visit, or the power of attorney has to be there to sign it. Two satisfaction paths, one blocker — and today neither state is recorded, so scheduling discovers the problem at the door. Proposed as S-43 (signature status, a rule-based flag the system can own and block on) and S-44 (the POA's own availability, a fluctuating human calendar like S-30).

    S-43 S-44 — proposed, not yet in the workbook

  6. 04

    Clinician restrictions

    GATE

    No SOC, no wound care, no high-acuity, territory limits. Documented firm restrictions that must hard-filter the match, not merely display beside it.

    S-21

  7. 05

    Patient & caregiver availability

    GATE

    Time-of-day refusals, day-of-week constraints, caregiver-present requirements, cognitive constraints, and a caregiver's own fluctuating schedule. Firm rules — but reported by people, and stale ones cause failed visits.

    S-25 S-27 S-28 S-29 S-30

  8. 06

    Clinical timing

    GATE

    Diagnosis-driven timing and competing medical appointments. Patient-safety judgment: the system flags, a person decides. Infection-control sequencing sits alongside as a softer rule.

    S-31 S-32 S-34

  9. 07

    Exceptions — the missed visit

    GATE

    Detecting the miss is clean; choosing the reschedule pulls in every soft constraint on this list. Missed-visit documentation is the compliance half.

    S-38 S-39

  10. 08

    Routing & geography

    MVPCONFLICT

    Home base, proximity to an existing route, optimised mileage, intra-day sequencing, and the appointment window. Deterministic and reversible — the strongest automation ground in this arena.

    S-14 S-17 S-18 S-19 S-20

  11. 09

    Patient preference

    MVP

    Gender preference, language and cultural match, preferred visit window. Gender is sensitive — surface and confirm rather than auto-match.

    S-23 S-24 S-26

  12. 10

    Clinician working pattern

    LATERCONFLICT

    Preferred days and start time, hard stop, lunch, documentation block, max daily volume, elasticity, overtime willingness. Almost entirely tacit today — and the rows where automating too hard damages the trust the schedule runs on.

    S-04 S-05 S-06 S-07 S-08 S-09 S-10 S-11 S-12 S-13

  13. 11

    Continuity of care

    LATER

    Same clinician, same patient. Drives trust and outcomes but competes with efficiency — a real trade-off leaders should set on purpose rather than let the optimiser decide.

    S-22

  14. 12

    Distribution across the week

    LATERCONFLICT

    Front-loading, pace against schedule, day-by-day balancing. A front-loaded week absorbs surprises; a back-loaded one turns any hiccup into missed visits.

    S-40 S-41 S-42

Making it actually happen

Patient Engagement

The communication and confirmation work that turns a schedule into delivered visits. CO-01–CO-12

  1. 01

    Availability confirmation before booking

    GATE

    Checking the patient and caregiver window before the visit is committed. Skipping it is how a technically valid schedule produces a failed visit.

    CO-06

  2. 02

    Call-out coverage coordination

    GATE

    One uncovered call-out cascades into a day of missed visits. How fast you backfill decides the damage — and coverage hinges on relational willingness, so surface options and let a person work it.

    CO-09

  3. 03

    Failed-visit follow-up & rebooking

    GATE

    The no-show loop. Detection is clean; the rebooking pulls the whole scheduling constraint set back in. This is where LUPA risk is won or lost.

    CO-08

  4. 04

    Day-before & same-day confirmation

    MVP

    The cheapest way to protect a delivered visit. The call also surfaces changes — so prompt it, and let a person make it.

    CO-01

  5. 05

    Automated appointment reminders

    MVPCONFLICT

    Rule-based dispatch on a known schedule. The cleanest automation win anywhere on this board.

    CO-02

  6. 06

    En-route notification

    LATER

    The sketch's “where’s my nurse?”. Derived from the live route, safe to automate, and the single thing patients judge reliability on.

    CO-03

  7. 07

    New-patient welcome call

    LATER

    Welcome and readiness before the first visit. Automate the trigger; the call itself is human and relational.

    CO-04

  8. 08

    Reschedule coordination

    LATER

    A relational negotiation. The system can propose slots; a person owns the outcome.

    CO-07

  9. 09

    Channel & preference management

    LATERCONFLICT

    Which patients take a text, a call, or an email — and confirming that choice for anything reliability-critical.

    CO-05

  10. 10

    Multi-discipline visit coordination

    LATER

    Sequencing nursing, therapy and aide visits against one patient's tolerance and several clinicians' routes.

    CO-10

  11. 11

    Care-team & office updates

    LATER

    Logging and notification automate cleanly; deciding what to escalate stays human judgment.

    CO-11

  12. 12

    Coordination time load

    LATER

    What all of the above costs the branch in hours. Measure and surface it — the discovery names the drowning scheduler as the real bottleneck, and nothing counts it today.

    CO-12
